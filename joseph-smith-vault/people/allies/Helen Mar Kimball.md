# 👤 Helen Mar Kimball

**Tags:** #person/ally #era/nauvoo #theme/polygamy
**Born:** August 22, 1828, Mendon, New York
**Died:** November 22, 1896, Salt Lake City, Utah
**Age at Sealing to Smith:** 14
**Sealed to Smith:** Spring 1843
**Father:** [[Heber C. Kimball]]

---

## Summary

Helen Mar Kimball was Joseph Smith's youngest documented plural wife, sealed to him in the spring of 1843 at age fourteen. Her sealing was arranged by her father [[Heber C. Kimball]], one of Smith's most loyal apostles, who presented the proposal to Helen as a spiritual opportunity that would bless their entire family. Helen later wrote extensively about her experience — providing one of the most detailed first-person accounts of what it felt like to be a plural wife in Nauvoo, including her initial resistance, her compliance, and her later complex loyalty to the principle.

---

## The Sealing

Smith approached [[Heber C. Kimball]] about sealing Helen. According to Helen's own memoir, her father told her that if she agreed, "it would ensure your eternal salvation and exaltation and that of your father's household and all of your kindred." Helen recorded that she consented after this assurance, though she described the decision as one of the hardest of her life.

The sealing was a **dynastic sealing** — a spiritual and eternal covenant — rather than a fully cohabitating marriage. Helen later stated she lived as a daughter in Smith's household, not as a conjugal wife, though historians debate the nature of the relationship.

---

## Her Account

Helen Mar Kimball is historically exceptional because she wrote about her experience at length, both defending plural marriage and honestly describing its psychological cost. Her memoir *Why We Practice Plural Marriage* (1884) and her poem written shortly after the sealing — expressing grief at losing her girlhood — are primary sources of unusual intimacy.

The poem included the lines:
> *I thought through this life, my time will be my own*
> *The step I now am taking's for eternity alone*

---

## After Smith's Death

After the martyrdom Helen was sealed "for time" to Horace Whitney and emigrated to Utah. She became one of the most articulate public defenders of plural marriage in the Utah period, writing extensively in response to anti-polygamy campaigns.

---

## Historical Significance

Helen Mar Kimball's case is central to any honest account of Nauvoo plural marriage. Her youth, the dynastic framing of the proposal, her own ambivalence, and her later written record make her the most documented of Smith's youngest wives.
