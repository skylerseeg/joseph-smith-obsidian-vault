# 👤 Parley P. Pratt

**Tags:** #person/ally #role/apostle #era/ohio #era/missouri #era/nauvoo
**Born:** April 12, 1807, Burlington, New York
**Died:** May 13, 1857, Van Buren, Arkansas (murdered)
**Occupation:** Author, Apostle, Missionary

---

## Summary

Parley P. Pratt was the most prolific author and arguably the most effective missionary in early Mormonism. He converted [[Sidney Rigdon]] to the church — an event that transformed the movement's geography and demographics — and went on to write foundational theological works that shaped LDS doctrine for generations.

---

## The Rigdon Conversion (1830)

Pratt was a former Campbellite preacher who joined the church in 1830 after reading the Book of Mormon. Almost immediately, he carried the book to his former mentor Sidney Rigdon in Kirtland, Ohio.

Rigdon's subsequent conversion triggered the mass movement of his congregation into the LDS church — one of the most consequential single missionary acts in the church's history. Without Pratt's 1830 Ohio mission, the church might never have established its Ohio base.

---

## Writing and Theology

Pratt was a gifted writer who gave Mormon theology much of its intellectual structure:
- **Key to the Science of Theology** (1855) — one of the most influential early LDS theological works
- **A Voice of Warning** (1837) — widely distributed missionary tract
- Numerous pamphlets, tracts, and letters that defined how Mormonism explained itself to outsiders

---

## Missouri Imprisonment

Pratt was arrested alongside Smith during the Missouri conflict and spent time in prison. He later wrote a detailed account of the imprisonment in his autobiography.

---

## Apostleship and Council of Fifty

Pratt was one of the original Twelve Apostles (1835) and a member of the **Council of Fifty**. He remained loyal to [[Brigham Young]] after the martyrdom and participated in the Utah exodus.

---

## Death

Pratt was murdered in Van Buren, Arkansas, in 1857 by the estranged husband of a woman who had converted to Mormonism and entered a plural marriage with Pratt. He died violently — a martyr-figure for the plural marriage controversy in the Utah period.
