# 👤 Brigham Young

**Tags:** #person/ally #role/apostle #era/ohio #era/missouri #era/nauvoo
**Born:** June 1, 1801, Whitingham, Vermont
**Died:** August 29, 1877, Salt Lake City, Utah
**Occupation:** Craftsman, Apostle, President of the Twelve Apostles, 2nd President of the LDS Church

---

## Summary

Brigham Young was Joseph Smith's most effective organizational lieutenant and his successor. While not Smith's closest theological confidant, Young was the man who held the church together after the martyrdom, organized the exodus to Utah, and transformed a persecuted frontier sect into an established territorial society.

---

## Relationship with Smith

Young joined the church in 1832 and rose steadily through the hierarchy:
- Appointed to the original Quorum of the Twelve Apostles (1835)
- Led multiple missionary expeditions to England, dramatically expanding membership
- Remained loyal through the Kirtland banking crisis when many apostles defected
- Managed the church's temporal affairs in Nauvoo — the operational and logistical engine behind Smith's prophetic vision
- Member of the **Council of Fifty**
- Accepted and practiced plural marriage — eventually marrying 55 wives

---

## The Succession Crisis (August 1844)

When Smith was killed, Young was in Boston on a mission. He rushed back to Nauvoo and faced [[Sidney Rigdon]]'s claim to guardianship.

In the "Succession Meeting" of August 8, 1844, witnesses reported that Young's voice and manner suddenly took on the quality of Smith's — an event described by many present as a miraculous "transfiguration." The congregation voted for Young and the Twelve.

---

## The Exodus (1846–1847)

Young organized and led the largest coordinated migration in American history — roughly 70,000 Saints from Nauvoo to the Great Basin over approximately a decade. He arrived in the Salt Lake Valley on July 24, 1847, reportedly declaring "This is the right place."

---

## Utah Period

- Served as the first Governor of Utah Territory (1851–1858)
- Came into conflict with the U.S. government during the "Utah War" (1857–1858)
- Formally reorganized the church after Smith's death; expanded the polygamy doctrine publicly (1852)
