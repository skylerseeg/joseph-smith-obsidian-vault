# 👤 Caleb Baldwin

**Tags:** #person/ally #era/missouri #era/nauvoo
**Born:** 1791, Connecticut
**Died:** 1849, Iowa
**Occupation:** Farmer, church elder

---

## Summary

Caleb Baldwin was one of four men imprisoned alongside Joseph Smith at Liberty Jail, Clay County, Missouri, from December 1838 through April 1839. He was a relatively senior church member — older than Smith by fourteen years — who had been present through the Missouri conflicts. His imprisonment alongside Smith during the most intellectually productive period of Smith's confinement makes him a direct witness to the conditions under which Smith composed the Liberty Jail epistles.

---

## The Missouri Crisis

Baldwin was caught up in the military and legal collapse of the Missouri Mormon community in late 1838. After the Battle of Far West and General [[Lucas]]'s surrender demand, a Missouri military court sentenced Smith, Baldwin, and several others to be shot. General [[Alexander Doniphan]] refused to carry out the order, calling it cold-blooded murder.

The men were instead imprisoned pending civil trial.

---

## Liberty Jail, December 1838 – April 1839

Baldwin was confined with Smith in the cramped, cold Liberty Jail for approximately five months. The other prisoners were [[Hyrum Smith]], [[Lyman Wight]], [[Alexander McRae]], and Sidney Rigdon (released earlier on health grounds).

During this confinement Smith composed long epistles to the church — letters that became some of his most theologically significant writing, later canonized in the Doctrine & Covenants. Baldwin was a daily witness to this period.

---

## Escape

The prisoners were allowed to escape in April 1839 while being transferred between counties. Missouri authorities appear to have facilitated the escape to avoid the political embarrassment of a trial. Baldwin made his way to Illinois with the other refugees.

---

## Nauvoo Period

Baldwin settled in Nauvoo and remained a loyal church member. He did not join the 1844 dissenter movement and was not involved with the [[Nauvoo Expositor]].
