# 👤 Heber C. Kimball

**Tags:** #person/ally #role/apostle #era/ohio #era/nauvoo
**Born:** June 14, 1801, Sheldon, Vermont
**Died:** June 22, 1868, Salt Lake City, Utah
**Occupation:** Potter, Apostle, Counselor in First Presidency (under Brigham Young)

---

## Summary

Heber C. Kimball was one of Joseph Smith's most loyal apostles — a man of intense spiritual conviction and physical courage who never wavered through any of the church's crises. He was an early participant in plural marriage (his daughter [[Helen Mar Kimball]] was sealed to Smith), a central figure in the Council of Fifty, and one of the original Twelve Apostles.

---

## Apostleship and Missionary Work

Kimball was called to the original Quorum of the Twelve Apostles in 1835. He led the first mission to England in 1837–1838 — an enormously successful undertaking that brought thousands of British converts into the church and fundamentally changed its demographic character.

He returned to England for a second mission in 1839–1841, again with remarkable success.

---

## Council of Fifty

Kimball was a central figure in the **Council of Fifty** — Smith's secret political body tasked with preparing for the literal "Kingdom of God." He was among the most trusted members of Smith's inner circle during the final Nauvoo years.

---

## Plural Marriage

Kimball was among the earliest initiates into the plural marriage system in Nauvoo. The sealing of his daughter **Helen Mar Kimball** (age 14) to Joseph Smith in approximately May 1843 is one of the most discussed and contested relationships in the plural marriage record.

Helen Mar later wrote about the sealing in terms that emphasized its dynastic and spiritual character rather than any marital cohabitation. She remained a devoted church member and wrote extensively in defense of the practice late in her life.

---

## Under Brigham Young

After the martyrdom, Kimball followed [[Brigham Young]] as one of his most loyal lieutenants:
- Made the exodus to Utah in 1847
- Appointed First Counselor in Young's First Presidency (1847)
- Died in Salt Lake City in 1868
