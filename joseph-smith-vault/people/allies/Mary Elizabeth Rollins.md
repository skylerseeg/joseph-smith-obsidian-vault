# 👤 Mary Elizabeth Rollins

**Tags:** #person/ally #era/nauvoo #theme/polygamy
**Born:** April 9, 1818, Massachusetts
**Died:** October 26, 1913, Utah (age 95)
**Sealed to Smith:** February 1842
**Status at sealing:** Married to Adam Lightner (polyandrous sealing)

---

## Summary

Mary Elizabeth Rollins Lightner was one of Joseph Smith's earliest and most theologically significant plural wives. She was already legally married to non-member Adam Lightner when she was sealed to Smith in February 1842 — making her sealing a case of **polyandry** (a woman sealed to a second husband while the first husband lives). She later claimed Smith had told her as early as 1834 that she was "chosen" for him before the world was. She lived to 95 and gave some of the most detailed late-life testimony about early plural marriage.

---

## Early Relationship

Mary Elizabeth first encountered Smith in Kirtland, Ohio in the early 1830s, when she was a teenager. She was deeply committed to the faith and had a reputation for courage — she and her sister rescued printed sheets of the Book of Commandments from a mob attack in Independence, Missouri in 1833.

She later described Smith telling her privately in the mid-1830s that she had been "given" to him by God before birth — an early indication of his developing theology of eternal sealing and predestination within plural marriage.

---

## The Polyandrous Sealing, 1842

Mary was sealed to Smith on February 1842 while legally married to Adam Lightner, a non-member who did not participate. This was not unique — several of Smith's sealings involved women in existing civil marriages — but Mary's case is among the best-documented.

She later explained her understanding: the civil marriage to Lightner was for time (earthly life only); the sealing to Smith was for eternity. This dual framework was how polyandrous sealings were theologically rationalized.

---

## Late-Life Testimony

Mary Elizabeth Rollins Lightner outlived nearly every other person connected to Nauvoo-era plural marriage. In the early twentieth century she gave sworn affidavits and public addresses about her experience, providing historians with unusually direct testimony about Smith's earliest plural marriage teachings. She consistently affirmed both the spiritual validity of her sealing and the psychological difficulty of living the principle in secret.

---

## After the Martyrdom

She remained with her civil husband Adam Lightner for the rest of his life, not emigrating to Utah until after his death. She eventually joined the Utah Saints and spent her final decades as a respected witness to early church history.
