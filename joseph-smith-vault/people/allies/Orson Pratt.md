# 👤 Orson Pratt

**Tags:** #person/ally #role/apostle #role/editor #era/nauvoo
**Born:** September 19, 1811, Hartford, New York
**Died:** October 3, 1881, Salt Lake City, Utah
**Occupation:** Mathematician, Apostle, Author, Publisher
**Relation:** Younger brother of [[Parley P. Pratt]]

---

## Summary

Orson Pratt was the most intellectually rigorous member of the original Twelve Apostles — a self-taught mathematician and astronomer who applied systematic philosophical reasoning to LDS theology. He published the **first printed account of the First Vision** in 1840 and produced a prolific body of theological and scientific writing throughout his career.

---

## First Printed First Vision Account (1840)

Pratt published a pamphlet in **Edinburgh, Scotland** in 1840 titled *A Interesting Account of Several Remarkable Visions* — the first time the First Vision narrative appeared in printed form.

This preceded all other published accounts, making it the founding document of the public First Vision narrative. Its publication in Scotland rather than America reflects the extraordinary reach of Mormon missionary activity in the British Isles.

---

## Mathematical and Scientific Work

Pratt was largely self-educated in mathematics and astronomy but developed genuine competence:
- Calculated the orbit of the 1843 Great Comet
- Wrote extensively on astronomy as evidence for LDS theological claims
- Attempted to mathematize LDS theology in works like *Absurdities of Immaterialism* (1849)

---

## City Councilor, Nauvoo

Pratt served on the Nauvoo City Council and was involved in the civic governance of the city during the final Nauvoo period.

---

## The Doctrinal Controversies

Pratt's independence of mind brought him into conflict with church leadership:
- Temporarily excommunicated in 1842 over personal and theological disputes with Smith
- Re-baptized and restored to apostleship
- Later clashed with [[Brigham Young]] over theological questions about the nature of God

These conflicts make Pratt an unusual figure — a deeply loyal church member whose intellectual rigor sometimes led him to positions the institutional church found uncomfortable.

---

## Legacy

Pratt published the first German-language account of Smith's visions through the work of [[Orson Hyde]] and contributed to the international distribution of LDS theology in the 1840s. He is considered the founding figure of LDS intellectual theology.
