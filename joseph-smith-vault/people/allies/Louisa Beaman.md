# 👤 Louisa Beaman

**Tags:** #era/nauvoo #theme/polygamy
**Born:** 1815
**Died:** January 15, 1850, Salt Lake City, Utah
**Sealed to Smith:** April 5, 1841
**Age at Sealing:** 26
**Status:** Single
**Evidence of Sexual Component:** Strong

---

## Summary

Louisa Beaman holds the distinction of being Joseph Smith's **first documented Nauvoo plural wife** — the sealing that marked the formal beginning of the plural marriage system in Nauvoo, distinct from the earlier Kirtland relationship with Fanny Alger.

---

## The Sealing

The sealing took place on **April 5, 1841** — conducted under an elm tree with Louisa disguised in a coat and hat. The officiant was **Joseph B. Noble**, Louisa's brother-in-law.

The outdoor location and Louisa's disguise reflect the extraordinary secrecy required — the plural marriage doctrine was known only to a handful of Smith's inner circle in 1841.

---

## The Noble Connection

Joseph B. Noble and his wife Mary Adeline Beaman (Louisa's sister) were among Smith's most trusted confidants in Nauvoo. Noble was one of the first men Smith approached about plural marriage, and it was Noble who performed the sealing to his own sister-in-law.

---

## Life in Nauvoo

Louisa lived in Nauvoo through the remainder of Smith's life, maintaining the secrecy required of plural wives. She did not live openly with Smith as a co-wife with [[Emma Hale Smith]].

---

## After the Martyrdom

Following Smith's death, Louisa was "sealed for time" (a living marriage arrangement) to Brigham Young — a common practice for plural widows of Smith in the post-martyrdom period. She emigrated to Utah with the main body of Saints and died in Salt Lake City on January 15, 1850, at approximately age 34–35.

---

## Historical Significance

The Louisa Beaman sealing is the **anchor date** for historians studying the Nauvoo plural marriage system. April 5, 1841 is the earliest documented, source-verified Nauvoo sealing, making it foundational to any timeline of the practice.
