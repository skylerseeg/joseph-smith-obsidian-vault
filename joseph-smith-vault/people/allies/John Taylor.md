# 👤 John Taylor

**Tags:** #person/ally #role/editor #role/apostle #era/nauvoo
**Born:** November 1, 1808, Milnthorpe, Westmorland, England
**Died:** July 25, 1887, Kaysville, Utah
**Occupation:** Editor (*Times and Seasons*), Apostle, 3rd President of the LDS Church

---

## Summary

John Taylor was the editor of the Nauvoo *Times and Seasons*, one of the four men present at Carthage Jail on June 27, 1844, and the only survivor besides [[Willard Richards]] who was actually in the room. He was shot four times and survived; a pocket watch stopped a fifth ball that would have been fatal. He later served as the 3rd President of the LDS Church.

---

## Editor of *Times and Seasons*

Smith took nominal editorship of the *Times and Seasons* in 1842, but Taylor did much of the actual editorial work. The paper was the church's primary intellectual and theological publication in Nauvoo.

---

## Carthage Jail — June 27, 1844

Taylor was in the upper room at Carthage Jail with Smith, [[Hyrum Smith]], and [[Willard Richards]] when the mob attacked:
- He was shot four times — in the thigh, arm, hip, and knee
- A fifth ball struck his pocket watch (which he had been carrying in his vest), deflecting it from his chest
- The watch, stopped at **5:16 PM**, is preserved as a historical artifact

After recovering, Taylor refused to cooperate with the 1845 murder prosecution, viewing it as a sham proceeding. His refusal deprived the prosecution of its most credible eyewitness.

---

## 3rd Church President

Taylor succeeded [[Brigham Young]] as president in 1880. His presidency was defined by the federal government's escalating anti-polygamy campaign. He died in 1887 while in hiding to avoid arrest under the Edmunds Act.

---

## Testimony Value

Taylor's survival and his subsequent lifelong refusal to recant his witness of Smith's prophetic calling are considered by LDS historians as significant evidence of sincerity — like [[Hyrum Smith]], he suffered for his testimony rather than gaining from it.
