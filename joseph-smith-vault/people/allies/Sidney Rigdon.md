# 👤 Sidney Rigdon

**Tags:** #person/ally #role/religious #era/ohio #era/missouri #era/nauvoo
**Born:** February 19, 1793, Saint Clair Township, Pennsylvania
**Died:** July 14, 1876, Friendship, New York
**Occupation:** Minister, First Counselor (LDS First Presidency)

---

## Summary

Sidney Rigdon was the most theologically sophisticated person to join Smith's movement in its early years. A former Campbellite (Restorationist) preacher with a large congregation in Kirtland, Ohio, his mass conversion in late 1830 brought hundreds of followers into the church and shifted its center of gravity to Ohio. He served as First Counselor in the First Presidency for over a decade — Smith's chief orator and theological co-laborer — before a spectacular falling-out over succession following the martyrdom.

---

## The Mass Conversion (1830)

When Parley P. Pratt carried the Book of Mormon to Rigdon's congregation in Kirtland, Rigdon's conversion triggered a cascade that changed the church's geography entirely. Within weeks, Smith received a revelation directing the church to gather to Ohio.

Rigdon brought:
- A built congregation of ~100 committed followers
- An existing communal economic infrastructure
- Theological sophistication shaped by Alexander Campbell's Restorationist movement

---

## Role in the First Presidency

- Appointed First Counselor when the First Presidency was organized (1832)
- Primary public orator for the church — known for passionate, sophisticated sermons
- Co-authored major theological documents including the "Lectures on Faith"
- At times, Rigdon's rhetoric got ahead of Smith's intentions — his "Salt Sermon" in 1838 (warning apostates they would be "trodden under foot") helped trigger the Missouri crisis

---

## The Missouri Period

Rigdon's **"Salt Sermon"** (June 1838) threatened dissenters in Far West with expulsion, inflaming tensions with both internal dissenters and external Missouri citizens. Historians view it as a significant escalation in the 1838 crisis.

He was arrested alongside Smith at Far West, imprisoned, and released (unlike Smith, who remained in Liberty Jail).

---

## Health Decline

Rigdon suffered from periodic mental illness throughout his career — described variously as "fits," "spasms," and episodes of what appears to be severe depression or psychotic breaks. These increased in frequency and severity through the Nauvoo period.

---

## The Succession Crisis (1844)

When Smith was killed, Rigdon — as First Counselor — claimed he should serve as "guardian" of the church. He was opposed by [[Brigham Young]] and the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles. In the famous "Succession Meeting" of August 8, 1844, the congregation voted overwhelmingly for Young.

Rigdon was subsequently excommunicated and formed his own splinter organization.
