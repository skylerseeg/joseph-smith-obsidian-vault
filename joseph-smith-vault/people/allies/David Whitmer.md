# 👤 David Whitmer

**Tags:** #person/ally #era/new-york #era/ohio #era/missouri
**Born:** January 7, 1805, Harrisburg, Pennsylvania
**Died:** January 25, 1888, Richmond, Missouri
**Occupation:** Farmer, Church Leader

---

## Summary

David Whitmer was one of the Three Witnesses to the Book of Mormon and one of the most significant early leaders of the church in Missouri — before his excommunication in 1838 during the Missouri conflict. Unlike many apostates, Whitmer never recanted his testimony of seeing the plates, making him one of the most studied witnesses in Mormon history.

---

## Role in the Translation

Whitmer's family provided the final translation venue:
- Smith and [[Oliver Cowdery]] completed the Book of Mormon translation at the Whitmer farm in Fayette, New York (June–July 1829)
- The Whitmer family — Peter Whitmer Sr., Christian, Jacob, John, Peter Jr., and David — became a foundational early convert group
- David was one of the Eight Witnesses who handled the physical plates

---

## One of the Three Witnesses

On June 28, 1829, David Whitmer, [[Oliver Cowdery]], and [[Martin Harris]] reported seeing the golden plates shown to them by an angel. Whitmer signed the formal "Testimony of Three Witnesses."

His account remained consistent until his death — he was interviewed dozens of times by journalists, historians, and curious visitors and never wavered on the core claim of having seen the plates in vision.

---

## Early Missouri Leadership

Whitmer was appointed president of the church in Missouri — a significant leadership role — during the early 1830s. He was a respected figure in the Missouri community and helped establish the Jackson County settlement.

---

## Excommunication (1838)

Whitmer was excommunicated in 1838 during the Missouri conflict. Charges included insubordination and refusing to accept Smith's prophetic authority. He relocated to Richmond, Missouri, where he lived for the remaining fifty years of his life.

---

## The Richmond Statement (1887)

One year before his death, Whitmer published *An Address to All Believers in Christ* — a pamphlet that:
- Maintained his testimony of the Book of Mormon
- Accused Smith of introducing "false doctrines" including priesthood hierarchy and polygamy
- Called for a return to original principles

Whitmer argued he left the *man* Smith, not the *book*.

---

## Death (1888)

Whitmer died in Richmond, Missouri, on January 25, 1888, at age 83. On his deathbed, he reportedly reaffirmed his testimony of the Book of Mormon to those present.
