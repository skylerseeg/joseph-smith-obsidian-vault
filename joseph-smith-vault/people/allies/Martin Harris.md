# 👤 Martin Harris

**Tags:** #person/ally #era/new-york #era/ohio
**Born:** May 18, 1783, Easton, New York
**Died:** July 10, 1875, Clarkston, Utah
**Occupation:** Farmer, Scribe, Financial Patron

---

## Summary

Martin Harris was the indispensable financial patron of the Book of Mormon. A prosperous farmer who mortgaged his farm to pay for its publication, Harris was also one of the Three Witnesses and served as an early scribe. His relationship with Smith was ultimately defined by the **lost 116 pages** — one of the most consequential events in early church history — and a later falling-out in Kirtland that lasted decades before a final reconciliation near the end of his life.

---

## The Anthon Transcript (1828)

Harris took a copy of characters Smith had transcribed from the plates to New York City to show to Professor **Charles Anthon** of Columbia College. Harris reported that Anthon said the characters were authentic ancient script; Anthon later denied this, saying he told Harris it was a fraud.

This episode fulfills a prophecy in Isaiah 29 ("a book that is sealed") in LDS interpretation.

---

## The Lost 116 Pages

Harris persuaded Smith to let him take the first 116 translated pages to show to his skeptical wife, **Lucy Harris**. The pages were lost (likely destroyed by Lucy). Smith reported that the plates were temporarily taken from him as a consequence.

This event caused:
1. A significant delay in the translation
2. A restructuring of the Book of Mormon's narrative (using the "small plates" of Nephi)
3. Lasting tension between Smith and Harris

---

## Financial Patron

Harris mortgaged his **farm of 151 acres** to pay the $3,000 printing cost for the Book of Mormon. He ultimately lost the farm when the mortgage was called. This sacrifice is central to his place in LDS hagiography.

---

## One of the Three Witnesses

On June 28, 1829, Harris reported seeing the golden plates in a vision along with [[Oliver Cowdery]] and [[David Whitmer]], shown to them by the angel Moroni. He signed the formal "Testimony of Three Witnesses" included in every edition of the Book of Mormon.

---

## The Kirtland Dissent (1837–1838)

Harris was among those swept up in the aftermath of the **Kirtland Safety Society** failure. He became a vocal critic of Smith in Ohio, at various points joining splinter groups.

---

## Final Reconciliation

Harris spent decades outside the main LDS church, investigating various splinter groups. In 1870 — at age 87 — he emigrated to Utah and was re-baptized into the LDS church. He died in Clarkston, Utah, in 1875 at age 92, never having recanted his testimony of the plates.
