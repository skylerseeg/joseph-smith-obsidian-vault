# 👤 Zina Diantha Huntington

**Tags:** #person/ally #era/nauvoo #theme/polygamy
**Born:** January 31, 1821, Watertown, New York
**Died:** August 28, 1901, Salt Lake City, Utah
**Sealed to Smith:** October 27, 1841
**Status at sealing:** Married to Henry Jacobs six weeks prior (polyandrous sealing)
**Later sealed to:** Brigham Young (1846, after Smith's death)

---

## Summary

Zina Diantha Huntington is one of the most theologically and historically significant of Joseph Smith's plural wives. She was sealed to Smith just six weeks after her civil marriage to Henry Jacobs — a case of polyandry that illustrates the radical disjunction between Smith's eternal sealing theology and conventional marriage. After Smith's death she became one of Brigham Young's plural wives and eventually served as the third general president of the Relief Society, making her one of the most institutionally powerful women in nineteenth-century Mormonism.

---

## Background

Zina's family was among the earliest and most committed Mormon converts. Her brother Dimick Huntington performed her sealing to Smith; her entire immediate family was deeply embedded in the Nauvoo inner circle.

---

## The Sealing, October 1841

Zina had married Henry Jacobs on March 7, 1841. Six weeks later she was sealed to Smith as a plural wife. Henry Jacobs, a faithful church member, apparently accepted the arrangement — though accounts of his feelings vary. Smith reportedly sent her the message: "Tell her... I have put it off and put it off until an angel with a drawn sword has stood before me and told me if I did not establish that principle... I would lose my position."

This account of angelic compulsion appears in several women's testimonies and represents Smith's reported justification for urgency in establishing sealings.

---

## Life in Nauvoo

Zina lived as a secret plural wife throughout the remainder of Smith's life. Henry Jacobs continued as her civil husband; Smith's relationship with her was conducted within the strict secrecy required of all Nauvoo plural marriages.

---

## After the Martyrdom

After Smith's death, Zina was sealed "for time" to Brigham Young in 1846, becoming one of his plural wives. Henry Jacobs was effectively displaced — Young sent him on a mission shortly afterward. Zina emigrated to Utah with the main body of Saints.

In Utah she became an institution in her own right:
- **Third General President of the Relief Society** (1888–1901)
- A leading public voice defending plural marriage during the anti-polygamy campaigns of the 1880s–90s
- One of the last surviving witnesses of Nauvoo-era polygamy

---

## Historical Significance

Zina's case is central to understanding the complexity of Nauvoo plural marriage — the coexistence of civil and eternal marriages, the role of angelic compulsion narratives, and the way plural widows of Smith were absorbed into Brigham Young's network after 1844.
