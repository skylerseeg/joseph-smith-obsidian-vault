# 👤 Lucy Mack Smith

**Tags:** #person/ally #role/family #role/chronicler #era/new-york #era/nauvoo
**Born:** July 8, 1775, Gilsum, New Hampshire
**Died:** May 14, 1856, Nauvoo, Illinois
**Occupation:** Homemaker, Chronicler

---

## Summary

Lucy Mack Smith is the primary biographical source for Joseph Smith Jr.'s childhood and youth. Her memoir, *Biographical Sketches of Joseph Smith the Prophet and His Progenitors for Many Generations* (1853), is one of the most important documents in early Mormon historiography — and one of the most contested.

---

## The Memoir

Published in 1853, nine years after Joseph's death and three years before her own, the *Biographical Sketches* covers:
- The Smith family's genealogy and New England roots
- Joseph Jr.'s childhood visions and the Moroni visitations
- The translation process and early church organization
- The Ohio and Missouri periods

**Historiographical status:** Invaluable for details unavailable elsewhere; also treated with caution by historians because it was composed decades after the events and shaped by Lucy's religious commitments and protective maternal instincts.

---

## Role in the Network

Lucy was not merely a bystander — she was an active agent in the early church:
- Participated in early prayer circles and testimony meetings
- Maintained the family's social network during the repeated relocations
- Outlived Joseph Jr. by 12 years, providing continuity between the founding generation and successors

---

## Death

Died in Nauvoo, Illinois, May 14, 1856. She remained in Nauvoo with [[Emma Hale Smith]] rather than following [[Brigham Young]] to Utah.
