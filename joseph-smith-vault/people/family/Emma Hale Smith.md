# 👤 Emma Hale Smith

**Tags:** #person/ally #role/scribe #era/new-york #era/ohio #era/nauvoo
**Born:** July 10, 1804, Harmony, Pennsylvania
**Died:** April 30, 1879, Nauvoo, Illinois
**Married:** January 17, 1827 (legal, civil)

---

## Summary

Emma Hale Smith was the only woman Joseph Smith married under civil law. She was his companion through every phase of his life — from the translation of the Book of Mormon to the violent end at Carthage — and her relationship to the practice of plural marriage remains one of the most psychologically complex narratives in Mormon history.

---

## Early Life & Marriage

- Daughter of [[Isaac Hale]], a Pennsylvania farmer and hunter who was initially hostile to Smith
- Met Smith when he came to the Harmony area on a treasure-seeking expedition for [[Josiah Stowell]]
- Eloped with Smith on January 17, 1827, against her father's wishes
- Quickly became essential to the early translation work

---

## Role as Scribe

Emma was one of Smith's earliest and most important scribes, preceding [[Oliver Cowdery]]. She transcribed portions of the Book of Mormon text and later described the process in detail — she could not see any manuscript or book when Smith dictated.

---

## Relief Society

Emma was elected the first president of the Female Relief Society of Nauvoo in 1842, a position of significant institutional authority within the church.

---

## Plural Marriage: A Contested History

Emma's relationship to polygamy was deeply ambivalent and evolved throughout Smith's lifetime:

- Initially unaware of the Fanny Alger relationship in Kirtland (~1833–1836)
- In Nauvoo, Smith eventually revealed the plural marriage revelation to her
- She gave consent for some sealings — including to the [[Eliza Maria Partridge]] and [[Emily Dow Partridge]] sisters — before withdrawing that consent
- She reportedly destroyed a written copy of the plural marriage revelation
- Her facilitation and then withdrawal of consent directly fueled tensions that fed the *Nauvoo Expositor* accusations

> The domestic volatility within the Smith household during 1843–1844 was directly witnessed by the Fosters and Laws, and became a core grievance in the *Expositor*.

---

## After Martyrdom

- Refused to follow [[Brigham Young]] to Utah
- Remained in Nauvoo
- Joined the Reorganized Church of Jesus Christ of Latter Day Saints (RLDS) — now Community of Christ — under her son Joseph Smith III
- Publicly denied that Joseph practiced plural marriage until her death
- Died in the Nauvoo Mansion House, April 30, 1879
