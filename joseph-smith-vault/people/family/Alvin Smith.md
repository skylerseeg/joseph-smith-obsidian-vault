# 👤 Alvin Smith

**Tags:** #person/ally #role/family #era/new-york
**Born:** February 11, 1798, Tunbridge, Vermont
**Died:** November 19, 1823, Palmyra, New York
**Age at Death:** 25

---

## Summary

Alvin Smith was the eldest brother of Joseph Smith Jr. and, despite dying before the church was organized, played a pivotal spiritual role in the family's prophetic narrative. His early death became a defining event in Joseph's theological development.

---

## Life

- Eldest of the Smith children; described as industrious, talented, and the most capable of the brothers
- Supervised construction of the family's first permanent home in Palmyra, NY — a log cabin he largely built himself
- Was planning to help fund Joseph's visionary work before his death
- Not a member of any church at the time of his death

---

## Death and Its Significance

Alvin died on November 19, 1823, from what was likely a severe abdominal obstruction. A local physician gave him calomel (mercury chloride), which may have hastened his death.

His death had two major consequences:

1. **Spiritual:** Joseph later reported a vision in which he saw Alvin in the celestial kingdom — despite Alvin never having been baptized. This became the doctrinal seed for **baptism for the dead** (proxy baptism), introduced in Nauvoo in 1840–1841.

2. **Social:** Alvin had been the family's economic anchor. His death increased financial pressure on the family, deepening their dependence on treasure-seeking income and, eventually, on the patronage of [[Martin Harris]] and [[Joseph Knight Sr.]]

---

## The Grave Controversy

A local newspaper report suggested Alvin's body had been disinterred and dissected by a physician. Joseph Smith Sr. took out a newspaper notice denying this and confirming the body was undisturbed. The episode reflects the family's precarious social standing in Palmyra.
