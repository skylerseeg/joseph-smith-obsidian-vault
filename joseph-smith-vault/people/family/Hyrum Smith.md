# 👤 Hyrum Smith

**Tags:** #person/ally #role/family #era/new-york #era/ohio #era/missouri #era/nauvoo
**Born:** February 9, 1800, Tunbridge, Vermont
**Died:** June 27, 1844, Carthage, Illinois (assassinated)
**Occupation:** Farmer, Church Patriarch, Assistant President

---

## Summary

Hyrum Smith was Joseph's closest lifelong ally — his older brother, co-martyr, and perhaps the only person who never wavered in his loyalty through every crisis from Palmyra to Carthage. He died in the same volley of gunfire that killed Joseph, making him a "co-martyr" in Latter-day Saint tradition.

---

## Role Throughout Smith's Career

### New York / Pennsylvania
- Participated in the early treasure-seeking activities alongside his father
- One of the Eight Witnesses to the golden plates (handled them physically)
- A founding member of the church on April 6, 1830

### Ohio / Kirtland
- Served in multiple leadership capacities
- Remained loyal during the Kirtland Safety Society collapse when many others defected

### Missouri
- Arrested with Joseph at Far West in 1838
- Shared imprisonment — including time at Liberty Jail
- Escaped with Joseph to Illinois in 1839

### Nauvoo
- Appointed **Assistant President of the Church** (1841) — formally second only to Joseph
- Served as **Church Patriarch**
- Accepted the plural marriage revelation and was sealed to multiple wives
- Surrendered to Carthage with Joseph in June 1844

---

## The Martyrdom — June 27, 1844

Hyrum was shot first. A ball struck him in the face as the mob rushed Carthage Jail. He died almost instantly, reportedly saying "I am a dead man." Joseph, upon seeing Hyrum fall, reportedly cried "Oh dear, brother Hyrum!"

Joseph was killed moments later attempting to jump from the second-story window.

---

## Legacy

Hyrum's death alongside Joseph is central to the "martyrdom" narrative in LDS theology. His loyalty is used as evidence of the sincerity of both brothers' convictions — a man does not die for something he knows to be a fraud.
