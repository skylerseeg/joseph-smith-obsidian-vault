# 🏛️ Thomas Reynolds

**Tags:** #person/foe #role/politician #era/nauvoo
**Born:** March 12, 1796, Scott County, Kentucky
**Died:** February 9, 1844, Jefferson City, Missouri (suicide)
**Occupation:** Governor of Missouri (1840–1844), Lawyer

---

## Summary

Thomas Reynolds was the Missouri governor who persistently pursued Joseph Smith's extradition to Missouri throughout 1840–1844, ultimately failing due to [[Justin Butterfield]]'s successful defense before Judge [[Nathaniel Pope]]. His tenure is defined by the Mormon extradition issue and his tragic death — he shot himself in 1844, just months before Smith's own death.

---

## The Extradition Campaign

Reynolds inherited an ongoing legal problem from his predecessor [[Lilburn W. Boggs]]: Smith was in Illinois, charges against him in Missouri remained outstanding (treason, murder from the 1838 war), and Missouri demanded his return.

Reynolds pursued extradition through multiple channels:
- Issued extradition requisitions to Illinois Governor Thomas Carlin and later [[Thomas Ford]]
- Used the Boggs assassination attempt in 1842 to generate new extradition grounds (Smith as alleged conspirator)
- Maintained pressure on Illinois authorities over multiple years

**The key obstacle:** Reynolds either concealed or was unaware that the Missouri charges had been substantially undermined and that the legal basis for extradition was constitutionally dubious.

At the 1843 Springfield hearing, [[Justin Butterfield]] successfully argued that Smith was not a fugitive from Missouri justice because he had not been in Missouri when the alleged crime occurred.

---

## Death (February 1844)

Reynolds shot himself in Jefferson City on February 9, 1844 — approximately four months before Smith's own death. The reasons were reportedly depression and personal despair. His death ended the most persistent gubernatorial pursuit of Smith.

---

## Historical Context

Reynolds's obsessive pursuit of Smith across state lines reflects a genuine constitutional tension of the antebellum period: states' rights doctrine meant Missouri had limited ability to compel Illinois to extradite, while the Mormon community's organized legal defense was sophisticated enough to exploit every constitutional protection available.
