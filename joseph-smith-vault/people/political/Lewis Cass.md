# 👤 Lewis Cass

**Tags:** #person/neutral #era/nauvoo #role/politician
**Born:** October 9, 1782, New Hampshire
**Died:** June 17, 1866, Detroit, Michigan
**Occupation:** U.S. Senator (Michigan), Secretary of War, Secretary of State, presidential candidate
**Relation to Smith:** Recipient of petition; no personal relationship

---

## Summary

Lewis Cass was one of the most prominent American politicians of the antebellum era — U.S. Secretary of War, Senator from Michigan, 1848 Democratic presidential candidate, and later Secretary of State under Buchanan. His connection to Joseph Smith is indirect but historically significant: Smith and the Nauvoo Saints petitioned Cass as Secretary of War in 1839–1840, seeking federal redress for the Missouri expulsion. Cass's refusal to act — on constitutional grounds that the federal government could not intervene in state affairs — was one of a series of rejections that pushed Smith toward the conclusion that the Saints needed their own political power.

---

## The Missouri Redress Petitions

After the Saints were expelled from Missouri in 1839, Smith and other church leaders traveled to Washington D.C. to seek federal assistance. They met with President Martin Van Buren and members of Congress. Smith also directed petitions to Cass as Secretary of War, requesting either military protection or federal compensation for property losses.

Cass, like Van Buren, declined. The standard response was that the federal government lacked constitutional authority to intervene in what was a matter between Missouri citizens and the state government.

---

## Significance for Smith's Political Theology

The Washington failure — including Cass's rejection — was a turning point in Smith's political thinking. The experience that the existing American political system would not protect a religious minority accelerated his development of the [[Council of Fifty]] as an independent political body and his 1844 presidential campaign as a vehicle for proposing systemic change.

Smith's *General Smith's Views of the Powers and Policy of the Government of the United States* (1844) can be read partly as a response to the institutional failures embodied by men like Cass.

---

## 1848 Presidential Race

Cass was the Democratic nominee in 1848, losing to Zachary Taylor. He is associated with the doctrine of **popular sovereignty** on slavery — letting territories decide for themselves — which became the framework of the Kansas-Nebraska Act. He had no further connection to Mormon affairs.
