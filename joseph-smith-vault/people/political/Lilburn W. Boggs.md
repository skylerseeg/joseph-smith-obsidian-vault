# 🏛️ Lilburn W. Boggs

**Tags:** #person/foe #role/politician #era/missouri
**Born:** December 14, 1796, Lexington, Kentucky
**Died:** March 14, 1861, Napa, California
**Occupation:** Merchant, Lt. Governor, Governor of Missouri (1836–1840)

---

## Summary

Lilburn W. Boggs issued **Missouri Executive Order 44** on October 27, 1838 — the "Extermination Order" — which directed the Missouri state militia to treat the Mormons as enemies and either exterminate them or drive them from the state. It is one of the most extraordinary documents of religious persecution in American history, remaining on the books until Governor Christopher Bond rescinded it in 1976.

---

## Missouri Executive Order 44 (October 27, 1838)

**Text of the critical passage:**
> "The Mormons must be treated as enemies, and must be exterminated or driven from the State if necessary for the public peace."

**Context:**
- Issued during the peak of the 1838 Mormon War
- Followed the Battle of Crooked River, in which Mormon militia attacked a state militia unit
- Reflected Boggs's view that the Mormons constituted a quasi-sovereign power threatening the state

**Immediate effect:**
- General [[Samuel D. Lucas]] moved on Far West with overwhelming force
- Smith and other leaders surrendered
- [[Alexander Doniphan]] refused the subsequent execution order
- Smith was jailed; the Saints were expelled from Missouri

---

## The Assassination Attempt (May 6, 1842)

Boggs was shot through the window of his home in Independence, Missouri, on May 6, 1842, by an unknown assailant. He survived but was severely wounded.

Missouri authorities accused Smith of conspiracy (based on the alleged role of Orrin Porter Rockwell as the shooter). This accusation became the basis for the 1843 extradition attempt that [[Justin Butterfield]] defeated before Judge [[Nathaniel Pope]].

Rockwell was eventually acquitted. The actual shooter was never definitively identified.

---

## Later Life

After leaving the governorship, Boggs emigrated to California during the Gold Rush and settled in Napa Valley, where he served as an alcalde (local magistrate) under Mexican then American law. He died there in 1861.

---

## Historical Assessment

The Extermination Order stands as the most extreme official act against the Mormons and is treated by scholars as evidence of how far frontier political and legal norms deviated from constitutional protections of religious freedom. It was not challenged legally at the time and remained unrepealed for 138 years.
