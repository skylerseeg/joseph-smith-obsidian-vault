## Key Causal Analysis

**Why did Knight and Stowell support Smith?**
Both possessed "surplus" resources — horses, paper, grain — required to sustain the translation. Their established farmer status gave them the economic cushion to take the risk of patronizing a young seer.

**Why did Chase and Stafford oppose Smith?**
They shared the folk magic subculture. When Smith transitioned from physical treasure-seeking to spiritual record-recovery, he broke the unspoken "money-digging social contract" — former partners viewed him as converting shared superstition into a private religious monopoly.

**The Harris paradox:**
Harris moved from skeptic of "idle gossip" to mortgaging his farm — illustrating the high financial stakes and genuine conviction that characterized the early inner circle.
