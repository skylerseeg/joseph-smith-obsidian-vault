# ⚖️ David Rice Atchison

**Tags:** #person/ally #role/lawyer #era/missouri
**Born:** August 11, 1807, Frogtown, Kentucky
**Died:** January 26, 1886, Gower, Missouri
**Occupation:** General, Missouri Militia; Lawyer; U.S. Senator (Missouri)
**Party:** Democrat → Pro-slavery faction

---

## Summary

David Rice Atchison occupies an unusual position in Smith's story — a non-Mormon Missouri lawyer and militia general who provided genuine legal protection for Mormon civil rights in the 1833–1838 period, then stood aside as the state apparatus crushed the Mormon community in 1838. His later career as a pro-slavery senator and role in "Bleeding Kansas" makes him one of the most historically significant figures in Smith's network.

---

## Legal Defense of Mormon Civil Rights (1833–1838)

Atchison was practicing law in Liberty, Clay County, Missouri, when the Mormons were expelled from Jackson County in 1833. He and his partner [[Alexander Doniphan]] took on Mormon legal defense work:

- Provided legal representation in multiple Missouri civil rights cases
- Served as **arbitrator** in disputes between Mormon settlers and Missouri citizens
- Attempted to use the courts to protect Mormon property rights and civil liberties
- Was sympathetic to the constitutional arguments about religious freedom

---

## The 1838 Conflict

Atchison was also a general in the Missouri militia. In the 1838 conflict, he occupied a complex position — commanding state forces while still being, technically, the Mormons' lawyer:

- Attempted to moderate the conflict
- Reportedly worked to prevent the massacre of Mormon settlements
- Was present during the negotiations that led to the Far West surrender

His ability to protect the Saints was ultimately overwhelmed by Governor [[Lilburn W. Boggs]]'s Extermination Order, which placed the state's full military power against the Mormon community.

---

## U.S. Senate Career

Atchison served as U.S. Senator from Missouri from 1843 to 1855. He was President pro tempore of the Senate from 1846 to 1849, which briefly made him the de facto president (Millard Fillmore's birth certificate issue aside).

He became a leading advocate for the **Kansas-Nebraska Act** (1854) and pro-slavery forces in the Kansas Territory — "Bleeding Kansas." His later career as a border ruffian organizer puts him on the opposite side of history from his early Mormon civil rights work.
