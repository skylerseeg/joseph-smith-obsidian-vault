# 👤 Josiah Quincy

**Tags:** #person/neutral #role/politician #era/nauvoo
**Born:** November 28, 1802, Boston, Massachusetts
**Died:** November 17, 1882, Quincy, Massachusetts
**Occupation:** Mayor of Boston, Author, Public Figure

---

## Summary

Josiah Quincy's visit to Nauvoo in May 1844 — just 45 days before Smith's assassination — produced one of the most intellectually sophisticated secular accounts of Smith ever written. His later essay in *Figures of the Past* (1883) is required reading for anyone seeking to understand how the American cultural elite perceived Smith at the height of his power.

---

## The May 1844 Visit

Quincy visited Nauvoo with **Charles Francis Adams** (son of John Quincy Adams and grandson of John Adams) on May 14–15, 1844. They were on a tour of the American West and stopped in Nauvoo as part of their itinerary.

Smith personally received them, showed them the city, and engaged in an extended theological and philosophical conversation.

---

## Quincy's Observations

From his later essay:

- Described Smith as managing a private militia of **3,000 men** and engaging in sophisticated multilingual and theological debates with visiting dignitaries
- Called Smith "a rare human being" — a man whose influence could not be easily dismissed even by skeptics
- Noted Smith's **physical presence** — tall, well-built, confident, with unusual personal magnetism
- Recorded Smith's claim that he would be elected president — a statement Quincy found simultaneously delusional and oddly credible given Smith's evident organizational capacity
- Found Smith's theology "monstrous" but could not dismiss Smith himself as a mere charlatan

---

## The Famous Assessment

Quincy later wrote:

> "It is by no means improbable that some future textbook... will contain a question something like this: What historical American of the nineteenth century has exerted the most powerful influence upon the destinies of his countrymen? And it is by no means impossible that the answer... may be thus written: Joseph Smith, the Mormon Prophet."

This is one of the most prescient assessments of Smith's long-term historical significance made by any 19th-century observer.

---

## Significance as Observer

Quincy represents the **"secular mirror"** category in [[The Prophetic Circle]]'s typology: journalists and observers who identified Smith as a "rare human being" even while labeling his doctrines "monstrous." His account is particularly valuable because:
- He had no religious stake in either direction
- He visited at the peak of Smith's Nauvoo power
- He was a sophisticated observer with extensive political experience (he became Mayor of Boston)
- His account was published nearly 40 years after the visit, allowing for reflection and perspective
