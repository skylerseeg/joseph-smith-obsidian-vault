# 📰 David Nye White

**Tags:** #person/neutral #role/editor #era/nauvoo
**Born:** Massachusetts
**Occupation:** Senior Editor, *Pittsburgh Weekly Gazette*

---

## Summary

David Nye White was one of the most significant journalists to interview Joseph Smith during his lifetime. His August 29, 1843 visit to Nauvoo produced a detailed, relatively sympathetic account that is now considered a primary source document.

---

## The 1843 Nauvoo Interview

- **Date:** August 29, 1843
- **Location:** Smith's sitting room, Nauvoo, Illinois
- **Duration:** Approximately one hour

### Key Observations
- White described Smith as possessing "amazing volubility."
- Noted that Smith's "whole theme was himself."
- Recorded one of the earliest published accounts of the **First Vision**, in which Smith described:
  - A religious reformation among various denominations in his youth.
  - Being led to James 1:5 for divine guidance.
  - Seeing "a glorious personage in the light, and then another personage."
  - Being told not to join any existing church because "they are all corrupt."

### Political Commentary
- White captured Smith's defiant political voice: *"None can get ahead of me, and few can keep behind me."*
- Smith vowed never to vote for a Democrat again, citing his mistreatment in Missouri.

---

## Significance

White was a sophisticated professional — not a partisan pamphleteer. His background in tax collection and politics gave him the analytical frame to present Smith as a complex public figure rather than a caricature. This makes the interview valuable as a secular, professional observer account.

> The *Pittsburgh Weekly Gazette* interview is one of only a handful of direct, first-person journalistic records of Smith's First Vision narrative.
