# 👤 Thomas Gregg

**Tags:** #person/neutral #era/nauvoo #role/editor
**Born:** 1808, Ohio
**Died:** 1892, Illinois
**Occupation:** Journalist, editor, historian
**Publication:** *Warsaw Signal* (intermittently); later Illinois local historian

---

## Summary

Thomas Gregg was an Illinois journalist and local historian who covered Nauvoo and Joseph Smith throughout the 1840s. Unlike [[Thomas C. Sharp]] of the *Warsaw Signal* — who became openly murderous in his rhetoric — Gregg was a more measured observer, though still critical of Mormon political power. He later wrote one of the earliest serious secular histories of the Mormon movement in Illinois: *The Prophet of Palmyra* (1890), which remains a useful primary-adjacent source for the Nauvoo period.

---

## Journalistic Coverage

Gregg covered the Nauvoo period for Illinois papers during the early 1840s. His reporting was skeptical of Smith's political ambitions and the concentration of power in Nauvoo but was generally less incendiary than Sharp's coverage. He was present in Hancock County during the events of 1844 and had personal observations of the political climate.

---

## Relationship to the Anti-Mormon Press

Gregg occupied a middle position in the anti-Mormon journalistic ecosystem. He shared concerns about theocratic governance with [[Thomas C. Sharp]] but did not publicly advocate violence. After the murders at Carthage he was among the journalists who covered the [[1845 Carthage Conspiracy Trial]].

---

## *The Prophet of Palmyra* (1890)

Gregg's 1890 book is one of the earliest attempts at a secular, historical treatment of Joseph Smith by someone who had lived through the Illinois period. It is useful for:
- First-hand observations of Nauvoo at its peak
- Contemporary Illinois political opinion on the Mormon question
- Coverage of the anti-Mormon movement and its press

It reflects the biases of its era and authorship but is more measured than the polemical literature of the period.
