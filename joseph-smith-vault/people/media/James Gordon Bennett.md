# 📰 James Gordon Bennett

**Tags:** #person/neutral #role/editor #era/new-york #era/nauvoo
**Born:** September 1, 1795, Keith, Banffshire, Scotland
**Died:** June 1, 1872, New York City
**Occupation:** Founder and Editor, *New York Herald*

---

## Summary

James Gordon Bennett was one of the most powerful newspaper editors in 19th-century America — founder of the *New York Herald* and a pioneer of the "penny press" revolution. His coverage of Mormonism, from an early 1831 report on "the Mormonites" through national coverage of the Nauvoo period, provided Smith with his most significant mainstream media legitimacy. Smith explicitly praised Bennett's coverage as "fair" and "high-minded."

---

## The *New York Herald*

Bennett founded the *Herald* in 1835 with $500 and a plank across two barrels as his first desk. It became the highest-circulation newspaper in the United States by the 1840s.

The *Herald*'s formula:
- Sensational crime and human interest stories
- Aggressive reporting that other papers considered undignified
- Genuine investment in foreign and national news gathering
- Political independence — neither Whig nor Democrat

---

## The 1831 "Mormonites" Report

Bennett's earliest Mormonism coverage predates the *Herald* — he reported on Smith's "treasure excavations" in New York in 1831. This early coverage documented Smith's folk magic activities for a national audience.

---

## National Coverage and Smith's Approval

By the Nauvoo period, the *Herald*'s coverage of the Mormon movement was substantial enough that Smith specifically praised it. The *Herald*'s national reach meant positive or neutral coverage there had outsized legitimacy-conferring power compared to regional papers.

Smith's description of Bennett's coverage as "fair" and "high-minded" — recorded in the research notes — suggests that mainstream national coverage, even from a sensationalist paper, was preferable to the hostile regional press of [[Thomas C. Sharp]] or the mockery of [[Abner Cole]].

---

## The Penny Press Context

Bennett's *Herald* was the paradigmatic penny press institution — proof that mass-circulation journalism could be both profitable and influential. The same media environment that allowed Bennett to build a national audience also allowed Sharp to build a regional mob. The technology was neutral; the editorial choices were not.

---

## Later Career

Bennett remained editor of the *Herald* until 1867, when he handed control to his son James Gordon Bennett Jr. He covered the Civil War, Abraham Lincoln, and dozens of other major stories. His 1872 obituaries described him as the most influential newspaper editor in American history.
