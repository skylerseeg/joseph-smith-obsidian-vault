# 📰 Eber D. Howe

**Tags:** #person/foe #role/editor #era/ohio
**Born:** 1798, Clifton, New York
**Died:** 1885
**Occupation:** Newspaper Editor, *Painesville Telegraph* (Ohio)

---

## Summary

Eber D. Howe was the author of *Mormonism Unvailed* (1834) — the first major book-length critique of Joseph Smith and the LDS movement. His work established the framework for 19th-century anti-Mormonism and remains a primary source document used (and debated) by historians today.

---

## Motivation

Howe's interest in Mormonism was personal before it was journalistic:
- His **wife and sister** joined the LDS church, which he viewed as a form of fraud and fanaticism
- As editor of the *Painesville Telegraph* in Ohio — near Kirtland — he had direct access to Smith's activities

---

## Collaboration with Philastus Hurlbut

The actual fieldwork for *Mormonism Unvailed* was conducted by **[[Philastus Hurlbut]]**, a disgruntled ex-Mormon who had been excommunicated for sexual immorality.

Hurlbut:
- Traveled to Palmyra and Manchester, New York, in 1833
- Collected **affidavits from Smith's former neighbors** painting the Smith family as lazy, superstitious, and dishonest "money-diggers"
- Attempted to locate and identify the source of the Book of Mormon (Spaulding Theory)

Howe compiled, edited, and published these materials as *Mormonism Unvailed*.

---

## *Mormonism Unvailed* (1834)

**Full title:** *Mormonism Unvailed: or, A Faithful Account of That Singular Imposition and Delusion*

**Core arguments:**
1. The Smith family were fraudulent "money-diggers" without social standing
2. The Book of Mormon was plagiarized from an unpublished manuscript by Solomon Spaulding (the "Spaulding Theory")
3. Joseph Smith was a conscious fraud, not a genuine visionary

**The Spaulding Theory** dominated critical Mormon scholarship for 50 years — until the actual Spaulding manuscript was rediscovered in 1884 and found to bear little resemblance to the Book of Mormon.

---

## Legacy and Assessment

*Mormonism Unvailed* created the **template for anti-Mormon literature** that persisted through the 19th century:
- Emphasis on the Smith family's poverty and "superstition"
- Neighbor affidavits as evidence of character
- Plagiarism theory as alternative origin for the Book of Mormon

Later scholars like Fawn M. Brodie (*No Man Knows My History*, 1945) drew on Hurlbut's collected materials while rejecting the Spaulding Theory.

The affidavits themselves are contested — LDS historians argue they reflect neighbor hostility shaped by the Smith family's religious claims, while critical historians treat them as evidence of the family's actual reputation.
