# 📰 Abner Cole

**Tags:** #person/foe #role/editor #era/new-york
**Pseudonym:** "Obadiah Dogberry"
**Occupation:** Editor, *Palmyra Reflector* (New York)

---

## Summary

Abner Cole was the first journalist to publicly mock and critique Joseph Smith and the Book of Mormon. Operating under the pseudonym "Obadiah Dogberry" in the *Palmyra Reflector*, Cole began publishing satirical pieces about Smith as early as 1829 — before the Book of Mormon was even published — making him the originating figure of the anti-Mormon journalistic tradition.

---

## The *Palmyra Reflector*

Cole edited the *Palmyra Reflector* from a print shop that he rented on a part-time basis — the same print shop where the Book of Mormon was being typeset by E. B. Grandin. This proximity gave Cole direct access to advance excerpts of the text.

He used this access to:
- Publish excerpts of the Book of Mormon in his paper before its official release (which he was eventually legally forced to stop)
- Write satirical commentary mocking the text and Smith's claims
- Establish the "money-digger" and "imposter" narrative that would define anti-Mormon journalism for decades

---

## "Obadiah Dogberry"

Cole's pseudonym became the voice of the first sustained public mockery of Smith:
- Portrayed Smith as a charlatan exploiting rural credulity
- Ridiculed the Book of Mormon's language and claims
- Published negative neighbor testimony years before [[Philastus Hurlbut]] organized his formal affidavit campaign

---

## The Media-Legal Cycle: Originating Node

Cole's *Reflector* pieces are the starting point of the **media-legal cycle** described in [[The Media the Law and the Prophet]]:

> "Abner Cole's mockery in New York fueled local prejudice that manifested in the 1830 trials."

The cultural atmosphere Cole created in Palmyra — one of ridicule and suspicion — made it easier for local magistrates and juries to view Smith as a criminal rather than a religious figure.

---

## Legacy

Cole's work was relatively amateurish and local, but its timing made it foundational. He established the *genre* of anti-Mormon journalism — satirical, contemptuous, and rooted in neighbor testimony — that later figures like [[Thomas C. Sharp]] would refine into a far more dangerous instrument.
