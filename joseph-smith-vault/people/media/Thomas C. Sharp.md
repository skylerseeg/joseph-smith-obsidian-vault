# 📰 Thomas C. Sharp

**Tags:** #person/foe #role/editor #era/nauvoo
**Occupation:** Editor, *Warsaw Signal*; Organizer, Anti-Mormon Political Party
**Trial Status:** Indicted for murder of Joseph Smith, 1844. Acquitted, May 30, 1845.

---

## Summary

Thomas C. Sharp was the single most consequential media figure in Joseph Smith's assassination. As editor of the *Warsaw Signal*, he transformed local civil grievances into a statewide crisis through a campaign of inflammatory editorials that framed violence against the Mormons as a patriotic duty.

---

## Role in the Media-Legal Cycle

Sharp is the clearest example of the "media-legal cycle" described in [[The Media the Law and the Prophet]]:

> His editorials made an impartial jury in Hancock County impossible, ensuring the acquittal of the very men he helped organize.

1. **Press campaign** → Dehumanized Smith as a "hellish fiend"
2. **Mob organization** → Led the Anti-Mormon Political Party
3. **Murder** → Present at or participant in the June 27, 1844 attack
4. **Acquittal** → Benefited from the jury atmosphere his own press had created

---

## The *Warsaw Signal*

- Primary vehicle for anti-Mormon sentiment in Hancock County, Illinois
- Published editorials calling for the "extermination" of the Mormon leadership
- Created a regional media environment that made violence against Smith politically legitimate

---

## The 1845 Carthage Conspiracy Trial

**Case:** *People v. Levi Williams et al.*
**Presiding Judge:** [[Richard M. Young]]
**Defense attorneys:** [[Orville H. Browning]], William A. Richardson
**Prosecution:** [[Josiah Lamborn]]

Sharp was one of five defendants. He represented the editorial/organizational leadership of the conspiracy. The prosecution's case was severely undermined when Lamborn essentially disavowed his own star witnesses in closing arguments.

**Verdict:** Not guilty, May 30, 1845.

---

## Long-term Impact

The acquittal solidified the Mormon belief that justice was impossible under U.S. law, directly accelerating the 1846 exodus to the Great Basin under [[Brigham Young]].
