# 👤 Newel K. Whitney

**Tags:** #person/ally #role/business #era/ohio #era/nauvoo
**Born:** February 5, 1795, Marlborough, Vermont
**Died:** September 23, 1850, Salt Lake City, Utah
**Occupation:** Merchant, Bishop, Church Trustee, Land Agent

---

## Summary

Newel K. Whitney was the commercial anchor of Smith's Ohio period and one of the most important financial administrators in the church's early history. As a prosperous merchant in Kirtland, Ohio, he provided both physical space and business infrastructure for the church's temporal operations — and managed the church's complex financial affairs as trustee through the Nauvoo period.

---

## The Kirtland Foundation

Whitney owned a successful mercantile operation in Kirtland when Smith arrived in 1831. He became a convert and immediately became essential:
- Provided **office space** for Smith's administrative operations
- Managed the church's **mercantile interests** as part of the United Firm
- Served as the first **Bishop of Kirtland** — the ecclesiastical administrator responsible for temporal welfare

---

## The United Firm

Whitney was a key partner in the **United Firm** (1832–1834) — the joint-stock venture that managed church temporal interests in both Ohio and Missouri. This body handled land purchases, mercantile operations, and printing.

---

## Trustee in Nauvoo

When Smith reorganized church finances in the Nauvoo period, Whitney served as one of the primary **land agents and financial managers**:
- Handled thousands of land transactions as part of the church's Nauvoo real estate operations
- Managed the complex web of promissory notes and debt obligations that sustained the growing city
- One of the men who kept the church financially functional through the constant threat of bankruptcy litigation

---

## Plural Marriage Connection

Whitney's daughter **Sarah Ann Whitney** was sealed to Joseph Smith on July 27, 1842 — at age 17. Whitney facilitated this union, reflecting the deep personal trust between the two families. A letter from Smith to the Whitney family requesting secrecy around the arrangement is one of the more candid documents in the plural marriage record.

---

## Later Life

Whitney accompanied [[Brigham Young]] to Utah in 1848 and continued as Presiding Bishop until his death in Salt Lake City in 1850.
