# 📜 W. W. Phelps (William Wine Phelps)

**Tags:** #person/ally #role/scribe #role/editor #era/ohio #era/missouri #era/nauvoo
**Born:** February 17, 1792, Hanover, New Jersey
**Died:** March 7, 1872, Salt Lake City, Utah
**Occupation:** Printer, Editor, Political Clerk, Poet

---

## Summary

W. W. Phelps was the most politically skilled member of Smith's inner circle — a professional printer and editor who managed the church's publishing operations in Missouri and Ohio, served as Smith's "Political Clerk" in Nauvoo, and was the primary architect of the 1844 presidential campaign. He was also a gifted hymn writer whose compositions remain in the LDS hymnbook today.

---

## Early Career

Before joining the church in 1831, Phelps was already an experienced newspaper editor in New York. His professional skills were immediately put to use:
- Established the church's first Missouri press: **Evening and Morning Star** (Independence, MO, 1832)
- The press was destroyed by a mob in 1833, along with the Star's offices
- Re-established printing operations in Kirtland, Ohio

---

## The Missouri Period

Phelps was deeply involved in the Missouri conflicts:
- His 1833 article in the *Evening and Morning Star* about free Black people in Missouri may have been a triggering event for the first Missouri mob action
- He was excommunicated in 1838 during the Missouri conflict
- Wrote a letter to Smith asking for forgiveness — Smith's response is one of the most celebrated letters in LDS history: *"Come on, dear brother, since the war is past, / For friends at first, are friends again at last."*

---

## The "Political Clerk" (Nauvoo)

Re-baptized and fully restored, Phelps became Smith's most important political operative:
- Designated the **"Political Clerk"** — a unique title reflecting his unique function
- Drafted Smith's 1844 presidential platform: *"General Smith's Views of the Powers and Policy of the Government of the United States"*
- Managed correspondence with national politicians including [[Henry Clay]], [[John C. Calhoun]], and [[Martin Van Buren]]
- Helped coordinate the distribution of thousands of copies of the platform document across the country

---

## Hymns and Cultural Legacy

Phelps wrote dozens of hymns, many still used in LDS worship, including:
- "Praise to the Man" (about Joseph Smith)
- "The Spirit of God" (sung at the Kirtland Temple dedication)
- "If You Could Hie to Kolob"
