# 📜 Oliver Cowdery

**Tags:** #person/ally #role/scribe #era/new-york #era/ohio
**Born:** October 3, 1806, Wells, Vermont
**Died:** March 3, 1850, Richmond, Missouri
**Occupation:** Schoolteacher, Scribe, Lawyer, Editor
**Excommunicated:** 1838 | **Re-baptized:** 1848

---

## Summary

Oliver Cowdery was Joseph Smith's most important early collaborator — described in early church documents as the "second elder." He served as the primary scribe for the Book of Mormon translation, co-founded the church, and claimed to have received the Aaronic and Melchizedek priesthoods alongside Smith in 1829. His falling-out with Smith in 1838 and eventual reconciliation are among the most studied episodes in early Mormon history.

---

## The Translation (1829)

Cowdery arrived at the Harmony, Pennsylvania home of Joseph and [[Emma Hale Smith]] in April 1829 as a schoolteacher who had boarded with the Smith family in Manchester.

Within days, he became the primary scribe:
- Took dictation as Smith read from the plates (using the seer stone / Urim and Thummim)
- Transcribed the majority of what became the Book of Mormon
- Attempted to translate himself at one point but was told he failed due to lack of faith

---

## Priesthood Restoration (1829)

Cowdery and Smith reported that on May 15, 1829, **John the Baptist** appeared to them and conferred the Aaronic Priesthood. Shortly after, they reported receiving the Melchizedek Priesthood from Peter, James, and John.

These events are foundational to LDS claims of divine authority.

---

## "Second Elder" and Co-founder

- One of the Six Witnesses at the church's founding on April 6, 1830
- Described in early revelations as the "second elder" — institutionally second only to Smith
- Co-editor of the *Messenger and Advocate* (Kirtland, Ohio)
- One of the Three Witnesses to the golden plates

---

## The Break (1838)

Cowdery's excommunication in April 1838 was one of the most damaging defections in Smith's career. Charges included:
- "Virtually denying the faith" by claiming Smith's relationship with Fanny Alger was a "dirty, nasty, filthy affair"
- Refusing to submit to church authority in civil legal matters
- Attempting to sell his interest in the Missouri lands

His departure took with him intimate knowledge of the church's origins and founding events — knowledge he could weaponize against Smith.

---

## Post-Excommunication Life

- Studied law in Kirtland, Ohio, and was admitted to the bar
- Practiced law in Michigan and Wisconsin
- Never publicly denounced his testimony of the Book of Mormon, even while excommunicated
- Re-baptized into the church on November 12, 1848 — two years before his death
- Died in Richmond, Missouri, in 1850 at age 43

---

## The Documentary Legacy

As a scribe, Cowdery created thousands of pages of foundational church records. When he became hostile, he was the most dangerous possible defector — a man who had *written* the history and now held original documents.
