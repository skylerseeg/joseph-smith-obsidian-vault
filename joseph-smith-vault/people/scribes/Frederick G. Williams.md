# 📜 Frederick G. Williams

**Tags:** #person/ally #role/scribe #era/ohio
**Born:** October 28, 1787, Suffield, Connecticut
**Died:** October 10, 1842, Kirtland, Ohio
**Occupation:** Physician, Printer, Counselor (First Presidency)

---

## Summary

Frederick G. Williams was one of the most professionally credentialed members of Smith's early circle — a practicing physician who served simultaneously as Second Counselor in the First Presidency, co-owner of the Kirtland printing firm, and one of Smith's primary scribes during the Ohio period. His eventual dissent during the Missouri crisis and death shortly after make him one of the more poignant figures in the early church.

---

## Role in Kirtland

Williams brought a level of professional respectability unusual in Smith's early network:
- **Physician** — provided medical care within the Kirtland community
- **Printer/publisher** — co-owned the printing operation that produced early church publications
- **Scribe** — assisted in organizing and transcribing the early revelations
- **Second Counselor, First Presidency** — formal position of authority from 1833 to 1837

---

## Scribal Work

Williams assisted with several major documentary projects:
- Helped arrange the early revelations that became the **Doctrine & Covenants**
- Kept journals and correspondence during the Kirtland period
- Contributed to the earliest attempts to write a formal "History of the Church"

His work represents the transition from Smith's early oral prophetic style to the documented administrative record of the Kirtland institutional church.

---

## Dissent and Removal (1837–1838)

Williams was voted out of the First Presidency in November 1837 — a casualty of the Kirtland Safety Society crisis and the wave of internal dissent it produced. He was excommunicated in 1839 during the Missouri period.

Unlike many apostates, Williams was eventually re-baptized and reconciled with the church before his death in 1842 in Kirtland, Ohio — never having made the move to Nauvoo.

---

## Death

Williams died in Kirtland on October 10, 1842. He is buried there, one of the few founding-era leaders who never left Ohio.
