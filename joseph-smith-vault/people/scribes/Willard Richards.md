# 📜 Willard Richards

**Tags:** #person/ally #role/scribe #role/apostle #era/nauvoo
**Born:** June 24, 1804, Hopkinton, Massachusetts
**Died:** March 11, 1854, Salt Lake City, Utah
**Occupation:** Physician, Clerk, Apostle, Private Secretary to Joseph Smith

---

## Summary

Willard Richards was Joseph Smith's most important scribe in the final years of his life. As private secretary, he kept Smith's personal journals, recorded Council of Fifty proceedings, served as clerk to the Nauvoo City Council, and was the only person besides [[John Taylor]] present at Carthage Jail who was not killed — a fact he described as fulfillment of a promise Smith had made him.

---

## Role as Primary Scribe (1841–1844)

Richards arrived in Nauvoo in 1841 after a missionary tour of England and was immediately appointed Smith's private secretary:
- Kept Smith's **personal journals** — the most intimate documentary record of Smith's final years
- Served as clerk to the **Nauvoo City Council**
- Recorded proceedings of the **Council of Fifty** (the secret political body)
- Described in [[The Prophetic Circle]] as one of the "bureaucratic engines" who managed thousands of land transactions

---

## Carthage Jail — June 27, 1844

Richards was present in the jail cell with Smith, [[Hyrum Smith]], and [[John Taylor]] when the mob attacked. He survived with only a slight wound to his ear — a fact that became legendarily attributed to Smith's prophetic promise that "the ball would not hit him."

After the attack, Richards wrote the first account of the martyrdom, dispatching it to Nauvoo to prevent immediate retaliation.

---

## Documentary Legacy

Richards controlled the narrative of Smith's final years more than any other individual:
- His journals became the foundation of Smith's official "History of the Church"
- He continued as church historian under [[Brigham Young]]
- Accompanied Young to Utah in 1847
