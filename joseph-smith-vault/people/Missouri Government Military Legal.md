## Structural Analysis

### Why Missouri Failed to Execute Smith

The execution order chain illustrates how individual conscience intersected with military hierarchy:

```
Governor Boggs → Extermination Order
    ↓
Major General Lucas → Execution Order
    ↓
General Doniphan → REFUSED ("Cold-blooded murder")
    ↓
Smith's life spared
```

Without Doniphan's refusal, Smith would have been shot by firing squad on the morning of November 2, 1838 — fifteen years before the church reached Utah.

### The "Paramilitary Necessity" Dynamic

Smith's network in Missouri was inherently paramilitary because the civil system had already failed:
- Jackson County expulsion (1833): civil authorities failed to protect
- Clay County (1834–1836): temporary tolerance only
- Caldwell County (1836–1838): segregation without security guarantee
- Far West (1838): state militia as the threat, not the protection

Each failure drove the Saints toward more autonomous military organization, which in turn alarmed Missouri civilians and officials, completing a feedback loop that ended in war.

### Sterling Price — A Historical Footnote

Price's presence as a young militia officer guarding Smith is one of history's small ironies — he would later command Confederate forces west of the Mississippi during the Civil War, facing Union armies in the same Missouri landscape where he once guarded the Mormon prophet.
