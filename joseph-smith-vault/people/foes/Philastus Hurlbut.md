# 👤 Philastus Hurlbut

**Tags:** #person/foe #era/ohio #era/new-york
**Full Name:** Doctor Philastus Hurlbut (the "Doctor" was a given name, not a title)
**Born:** February 3, 1809, Chittenden County, Vermont
**Died:** 1883
**Excommunicated:** June 1833

---

## Summary

Philastus Hurlbut was an early convert turned bitter opponent whose fieldwork collecting anti-Smith affidavits provided the raw material for [[Eber D. Howe]]'s *Mormonism Unvailed* (1834). He also made death threats against Smith serious enough to trigger a legal proceeding.

---

## Background and Excommunication

Hurlbut (whose name was frequently mocked) joined the church in 1833 and was almost immediately excommunicated for **sexual immorality** — specifically, accusations of inappropriate conduct with women during a mission.

He appealed his excommunication but was rejected, transforming personal grievance into organized opposition.

---

## The Affidavit Campaign (1833–1834)

Hurlbut traveled to Smith's former communities in western New York — Palmyra, Manchester, Harmony — collecting signed statements from former neighbors:
- Witnesses like Willard Chase, Joshua Stafford, William Stafford, and others described the Smith family as idle, superstitious, and dishonest
- These statements were the primary "evidence" that the Smiths were "money-diggers" without credibility
- Hurlbut also sought to prove the Spaulding Theory — that the Book of Mormon was plagiarized

**The Spaulding Investigation:** Hurlbut located family members of Solomon Spaulding and obtained statements suggesting the Book of Mormon resembled Spaulding's unpublished novel. This theory dominated anti-Mormon scholarship until Spaulding's actual manuscript was found in 1884 and shown to bear minimal resemblance to the Book of Mormon.

---

## The Death Threat (1833–1834)

Hurlbut made threats against Smith's life serious enough that Smith filed a formal complaint. The hearing in **Painesville, Ohio** (1834) resulted in Hurlbut being legally restrained from contacting or threatening Smith.

Smith testified for three hours about his fears for his life, supported by attorney [[Benjamin Bissell]].

---

## Assessment

Hurlbut represents the "insider-turned-opponent" pattern that recurs throughout Smith's career. His excommunication gave him the motivation; his early church membership gave him the credibility to collect testimony from Smith's former neighbors who distrusted him. Without Hurlbut's legwork, *Mormonism Unvailed* could not have been written.
