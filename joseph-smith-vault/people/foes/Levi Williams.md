# 👤 Levi Williams

**Tags:** #person/foe #era/nauvoo
**Occupation:** Baptist Minister, Colonel, 59th Regiment, Illinois Militia
**Trial:** Defendant, *People v. Levi Williams et al.* (1845) — acquitted

---

## Summary

Levi Williams was the military commander of the mob that attacked Carthage Jail on June 27, 1844, allegedly giving the order that sent the armed men up the stairs. As Colonel of the 59th Regiment of the Illinois Militia, he lent military structure to what might otherwise have been a disorganized mob — making the Carthage attack a semi-coordinated paramilitary operation rather than a spontaneous riot.

---

## Background

- Baptist minister in the Warsaw/Green Plains area of Hancock County, Illinois
- Held the rank of Colonel in the Illinois state militia — a position of significant local authority
- A longstanding opponent of the Mormon presence in Hancock County
- Had personal grievances related to land and community influence

---

## Role in the Assassination

Williams is alleged to have:
- Organized and commanded the militia contingent that marched on Carthage Jail
- Given the order that sent men up the stairs of the jail
- Directed the charge that broke into the room where Smith, [[Hyrum Smith]], [[John Taylor]], and [[Willard Richards]] were held

Eyewitness **Jeremiah Willey** identified Williams as a key organizer.

---

## The 1845 Trial

Williams was the named defendant in *People v. Levi Williams* — the case title reflecting his perceived leadership role.

**Defense:** [[Orville H. Browning]] and William A. Richardson
**Outcome:** Acquitted, May 30, 1845

The combination of witness intimidation, eyewitness refusal by the Saints, and the poisoned jury pool created by [[Thomas C. Sharp]]'s press made conviction effectively impossible.
