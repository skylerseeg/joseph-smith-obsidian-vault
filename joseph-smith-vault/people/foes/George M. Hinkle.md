# 👤 George M. Hinkle

**Tags:** #person/foe #era/missouri
**Occupation:** Colonel, Mormon Militia (Missouri); Merchant; Minister
**Later:** Left the church; minister in various Protestant denominations

---

## Summary

George M. Hinkle was the Mormon militia commander at Far West, Missouri, who negotiated the surrender of Joseph Smith to the state militia in October 1838 — an act the Saints viewed as a catastrophic betrayal. He remains one of the most controversial figures in the Missouri period.

---

## The Far West Surrender (October 1838)

As the Missouri militia surrounded Far West with overwhelming force following the Battle of Crooked River, Hinkle — as the senior Mormon military officer — was tasked with negotiating terms.

**What Hinkle told Smith:** That the militia commander, General [[Samuel D. Lucas]], wished to meet with the Mormon leaders to negotiate a peaceful resolution.

**What actually happened:** When Smith and his companions walked out to meet Lucas, they were immediately seized as prisoners. Hinkle had negotiated their surrender without telling them that was what he was negotiating.

Smith reportedly felt that Hinkle had deliberately deceived him — luring him out under false pretenses.

---

## The Saints' Verdict

The Mormon community unanimously condemned Hinkle as a **traitor**. This verdict shaped how "betrayal from within" became a central interpretive lens through which Smith and his successors understood their persecutions.

---

## Hinkle's Defense

Hinkle maintained until his death that he had no choice — that armed resistance was impossible and that surrendering the leadership was the only way to prevent the massacre of the entire Far West community. He argued he saved thousands of lives, not betrayed a prophet.

---

## Later Life

Hinkle left the LDS church and eventually became a minister in the Disciples of Christ and other Protestant denominations in Iowa. He wrote several letters explaining his perspective on the Far West events, none of which satisfied his Mormon critics.

---

## Pattern Significance

In [[The Prophetic Circle]], Hinkle's surrender is identified as one of the two critical "betrayal as legal mechanism" events (alongside the *Nauvoo Expositor*) that transformed Smith from a religious leader into a legal defendant. The insider with legitimate access to Smith's trust and proximity was, in both cases, the one who delivered him to state power.
