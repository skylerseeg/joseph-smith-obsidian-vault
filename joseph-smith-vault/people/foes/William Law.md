# 🏛️ William Law

**Tags:** #person/foe #role/apostle #era/nauvoo
**Born:** c. 1809, Ireland
**Occupation:** Merchant; Second Counselor, First Presidency (LDS)
**Excommunicated:** April 1844
**Death:** 1892

---

## Summary

William Law was one of Joseph Smith's most trusted lieutenants before becoming his most dangerous internal opponent. As a wealthy Canadian merchant who rose to Second Counselor in the First Presidency, Law had more insider knowledge of Nauvoo's secret operations than almost anyone outside the polygamy inner circle — which is precisely what made his eventual opposition so devastating.

---

## Rise in the Church

- Joined the church in Canada; relocated to Nauvoo
- Appointed Second Counselor in the First Presidency — the #2 position in the entire church hierarchy
- Described as principled, financially successful, and genuinely devout

---

## The Breaking Point: Polygamy

Law's break with Smith came when he learned of the secret practice of plural marriage. He did not view this as a theological evolution — he viewed Smith as a **"fallen prophet"** who had corrupted the original church.

Key grievances:
- Objection to the polygamy revelation (Doctrine & Covenants 132)
- Claims that Smith attempted to proposition his wife, Jane Law
- Exclusion from the private inner network while holding a prominent public role

---

## The Reform Movement

Law did not simply leave — he attempted to reform the church from within:
- Founded the **Reformed Mormon Church** (also called the True Church of Jesus Christ)
- Organized with his brother [[Wilson Law]] and other prominent Nauvoo citizens
- The reform group included [[Robert D. Foster]], [[Francis M. Higbee]], [[Austin Cowles]], and [[Charles Ivins]]

---

## The *Nauvoo Expositor*

The reform group's most consequential act was publishing the ***Nauvoo Expositor*** on June 7, 1844 — its one and only issue.

The paper:
- Exposed the practice of plural marriage
- Accused Smith of theocratic abuse of the Nauvoo Charter
- Was published by individuals with firsthand knowledge of Smith's inner circle

Smith's response — ordering the city council to declare the press a "public nuisance" and having it physically destroyed — triggered the chain of events leading directly to his death.

See: [[Nauvoo Expositor]] · [[1845 Carthage Conspiracy Trial]]

---

## After Smith's Death

Law left Nauvoo and eventually settled in Wisconsin. He gave several interviews late in his life, including a notable 1887 interview with the Salt Lake Tribune, in which he maintained his belief that Smith was a fallen prophet but also expressed some ambivalence about the violence of June 1844.
