# 👤 Francis M. Higbee

**Tags:** #person/foe #era/nauvoo
**Born:** ~1820
**Died:** Unknown
**Occupation:** Merchant, Nauvoo
**Excommunicated:** 1844 (with the Law faction)

---

## Summary

Francis Higbee was the brother of [[Chauncey L. Higbee]] and one of the seven publishers of the *Nauvoo Expositor*. Where Chauncey was the legal strategist, Francis was the more publicly confrontational of the two — he had sued Joseph Smith for slander in 1844 after Smith accused him of syphilis and sexual misconduct in a sermon. That lawsuit, combined with the Expositor suppression, made the Higbee brothers the most legally active antagonists in the final weeks of Smith's life.

---

## The Slander Suit, 1844

In May 1844 Smith gave a public sermon at the Nauvoo stand in which he denounced Francis Higbee by name, accusing him of contracting syphilis and of sexual misconduct. Francis responded by filing a slander suit against Smith — adding another live legal proceeding to the growing stack of cases Smith was managing simultaneously.

The lawsuit contributed to the atmosphere of legal siege that defined Smith's final month.

---

## The *Nauvoo Expositor*

Francis was listed as one of the seven publishers on the June 7, 1844 masthead of the [[Nauvoo Expositor]]. After Smith ordered the press destroyed, Francis and [[Chauncey L. Higbee]] traveled to Carthage and filed criminal riot charges — the action that set in motion Smith's arrest and death.

---

## The Carthage Connection

The Higbee brothers' charges were filed in Carthage rather than Nauvoo, removing the case from Smith's legal control. Their choice of venue was deliberate and consequential: it forced Smith to surrender to Governor Thomas Ford and placed him in Hancock County custody, where the mob had access to him.

---

## After the Martyrdom

Francis was among the men indicted in the [[1845 Carthage Conspiracy Trial]] for Smith's murder but was acquitted along with all other defendants. He largely disappears from the historical record after 1845.
