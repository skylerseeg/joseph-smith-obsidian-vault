# 👤 Robert D. Foster

**Tags:** #person/foe #era/nauvoo
**Occupation:** Physician, Merchant
**Excommunicated:** April 1844

---

## Summary

Robert D. Foster was a physician and merchant in Nauvoo who moved from trusted associate to *Nauvoo Expositor* publisher. His personal grievance against Smith was among the most charged — he claimed Smith had attempted to seduce his wife, Jane Foster — giving his opposition an intensely personal dimension beyond the theological objections of the Law brothers.

---

## Background

Foster was a successful physician who arrived in Nauvoo in the early 1840s and established both a medical practice and commercial interests. He moved rapidly into Smith's circle of trusted associates, participating in church and civic governance.

---

## The Personal Grievance

Foster's break with Smith was fueled by the accusation that Smith had made romantic advances toward his wife, **Jane Foster**. This charge — if true — would represent Smith using his prophetic authority to solicit a married woman in his own inner circle.

The accusation was included in the *Nauvoo Expositor's* broader indictment of Smith's conduct and was one of the most inflammatory personal claims in the paper.

---

## The *Nauvoo Expositor*

Foster was one of the core publishers of the June 7, 1844 issue. His role was primarily as a financial backer and organizational participant — his brother **Charles A. Foster** also participated.

---

## Excommunication (April 1844)

Foster was excommunicated in April 1844 alongside [[William Law]] and others. The combination of his personal grievance, theological objections to polygamy, and objections to theocratic governance made him a fully committed opponent by this point.

---

## Significance

Foster represents the "intimate betrayal" dimension of Smith's opposition network. Unlike external critics like [[Thomas C. Sharp]] or [[Eber D. Howe]], Foster was inside the household — literally present at social events and privy to the domestic tensions between Smith and [[Emma Hale Smith]] over polygamy.
