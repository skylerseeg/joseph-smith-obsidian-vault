# 👤 Chauncey L. Higbee

**Tags:** #person/foe #era/nauvoo #role/lawyer
**Born:** 1821, Ohio
**Died:** 1884
**Occupation:** Lawyer, Nauvoo
**Excommunicated:** May 1842

---

## Summary

Chauncey Higbee was a young Nauvoo lawyer and son of Elias Higbee (one of Smith's trusted agents in Washington). He was excommunicated in 1842 on charges related to sexual misconduct — allegedly using plural marriage doctrine as cover to seduce women — and became one of the most bitter dissenters in Nauvoo. Two years later he co-published the *Nauvoo Expositor*, the newspaper whose suppression triggered Smith's arrest and death.

---

## Background

Chauncey came to Nauvoo with strong Mormon credentials. His father [[Elias Higbee]] was a church historian and close associate of Smith who had lobbied Congress on behalf of Missouri Mormon refugees. Chauncey studied law and set up practice in Nauvoo.

---

## Excommunication, 1842

Chauncey was excommunicated in May 1842, the same wave that removed [[John C. Bennett]] from fellowship. The charges involved seducing women by claiming plural marriage authority he did not possess. He denied the charges but the church court ruled against him.

The excommunication made him permanently hostile to Smith and aligned him with the growing dissident community centered on [[John C. Bennett]] and later [[William Law]].

---

## The *Nauvoo Expositor*, 1844

Higbee was one of the seven publishers listed on the masthead of the *Nauvoo Expositor* (June 7, 1844). The paper alleged:
- Smith was a fallen prophet practicing unauthorized polygamy
- The Nauvoo city government was a theocratic tyranny
- The [[Council of Fifty]] planned political domination

When Smith ordered the press destroyed as a public nuisance, Higbee and his brother [[Francis M. Higbee]] filed riot charges — the legal mechanism that ultimately brought Smith to Carthage Jail.

---

## Legal Role in Smith's Death

After the press destruction, Chauncey and Francis filed criminal complaints against Smith in Carthage, Hancock County — outside Nauvoo's jurisdiction. These charges were the proximate legal cause of Smith's surrender to Governor Ford and his subsequent murder at Carthage Jail on June 27, 1844.

---

## After the Martyrdom

Chauncey remained in Illinois. He was indicted as a co-conspirator in the [[1845 Carthage Conspiracy Trial]] but, like all defendants, was acquitted. He later practiced law in Illinois and Missouri.
