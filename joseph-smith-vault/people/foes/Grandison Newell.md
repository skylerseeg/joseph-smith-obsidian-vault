# 👤 Grandison Newell

**Tags:** #person/foe #era/ohio
**Occupation:** Businessman, Justice of the Peace (Ohio)

---

## Summary

Grandison Newell was Joseph Smith's most persistent legal antagonist during the Ohio period — a Kirtland businessman who prosecuted Smith and [[Sidney Rigdon]] for illegal banking, reportedly attempted to goad Smith into violence, and represented the commercial establishment's hostility to Mormon economic power.

---

## Background

Newell was a successful businessman and minor public official in the Kirtland/Mentor, Ohio area. His opposition to Smith was both personal and commercial — he viewed the Mormon presence in Kirtland as a competitor to established local business interests and considered Smith's banking venture a fraudulent threat to his neighbors.

---

## The Illegal Banking Prosecution

When Smith and Rigdon proceeded with the **Kirtland Safety Society Anti-Banking Company** without a state charter, Newell filed the prosecution that forced them to flee Ohio:
- Used his connections in the Ohio legislature to block the bank charter in the first place
- Filed criminal charges for illegal banking when they proceeded anyway
- The charges forced Smith and Rigdon to flee Kirtland in January 1838

---

## The Murder Plot Accusation (1837)

In 1837, Newell accused Smith of conspiring to have him murdered — an extraordinary charge that reflected the intense personal animosity between them. Smith denied the accusation.

The charge was never prosecuted successfully, but it contributed to the atmosphere of legal threat that drove Smith out of Ohio.

---

## Pattern Significance

Newell represents a consistent pattern in Smith's career: the commercial establishment figure who used the legal system instrumentally against Mormon economic competition. He was not a religious opponent — he was a businessman protecting market territory.

The [[Kirtland Safety Society]] failure was partly his achievement: he blocked the charter, then prosecuted the illegal alternative. This pattern of using legal mechanisms to destroy Mormon economic institutions recurred throughout Smith's career.
