# 👤 John C. Bennett

**Tags:** #person/foe #role/politician #era/nauvoo
**Born:** August 3, 1804, Fairhaven, Massachusetts
**Died:** August 5, 1867, Polk City, Iowa
**Occupation:** Physician, General, Mayor of Nauvoo, Author
**Excommunicated:** May 1842

---

## Summary

John C. Bennett was perhaps the most dangerous single defector in Smith's career. A physician and political operator of extraordinary skill, he was the primary architect of the Nauvoo Charter, Smith's first mayor, and a major general of the Nauvoo Legion — before his excommunication in 1842 transformed him into the church's most damaging public critic.

---

## Rise to Power in Nauvoo

Bennett arrived in Nauvoo in 1840 with impressive credentials — physician, former Quartermaster General of Illinois, skilled political networker. Smith quickly recognized his utility:

- **Primary author of the Nauvoo Charter** — the remarkable document that gave Nauvoo extraordinary municipal powers, including a habeas corpus provision that Smith used repeatedly to block extradition
- **First Mayor of Nauvoo** (1841–1842)
- **Major General of the Nauvoo Legion**
- Member of Smith's inner circle; initiated into plural marriage doctrine

---

## The Exposure

Bennett was excommunicated in May 1842. The charges against him included:
- Seducing women by claiming Smith had authorized "spiritual wifery" as a cover for ordinary adultery
- Having a wife and children in Ohio he had abandoned (bigamy in the civil sense)
- "Conduct unbecoming a gentleman"

Smith and Bennett's supporters framed it as Bennett exploiting the sacred plural marriage doctrine for personal predation. Bennett framed it as Smith's system being inherently exploitative and his own excommunication as retaliation for threatening to expose it.

---

## *History of the Saints* (1842)

Bennett published a sensational book-length expose:
- Alleged that Smith's "spiritual wifery" was a system of coerced sexual exploitation
- Described the Nauvoo Legion as a personal army for theocratic conquest
- Accused Smith of plotting to create a sovereign Mormon kingdom

The book was national news and shaped public perception of the Nauvoo theocracy more than any other single document of the period.

---

## Legacy

Bennett is among the most difficult figures in Mormon history to assess. He was genuinely brilliant and contributed real institutional infrastructure to Nauvoo. He was also genuinely unscrupulous — the abandoned wife and children are documented. His accusations against Smith mix verifiable facts (plural marriage existed) with inflammatory embellishments (the extent of coercion). Historians continue to disagree on the proper weight to give his testimony.
