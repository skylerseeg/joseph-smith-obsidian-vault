# 👤 Charles Ivins

**Tags:** #person/foe #era/nauvoo
**Born:** 1799, New Jersey
**Died:** Unknown
**Occupation:** Merchant, Nauvoo
**Excommunicated:** 1844

---

## Summary

Charles Ivins was a Nauvoo merchant and one of the seven publishers of the *Nauvoo Expositor*. He came from a New Jersey Quaker background and had converted to Mormonism in the 1830s, following the faith through its Ohio and Missouri periods before settling in Nauvoo. His break with Smith in 1844 was driven primarily by opposition to plural marriage and what he perceived as the corrupting effect of theocratic consolidation on the original egalitarian promise of the church.

---

## Background

Ivins was among the committed early converts who had sacrificed substantially for the Mormon cause — enduring the expulsion from Missouri and the hardships of the early Nauvoo period. This made his disillusionment particularly pointed: he was not a casual convert but a long-term believer who felt the church had departed from its founding principles.

---

## The *Nauvoo Expositor*

Ivins was listed as one of the seven publishers on the [[Nauvoo Expositor]] masthead. His contribution was primarily financial and organizational — he provided resources and credibility to the dissenter coalition led by [[William Law]].

---

## After the Martyrdom

Ivins did not rejoin the LDS church. He largely disappears from the historical record after 1844, having left Nauvoo following Smith's death.

---

## Family Note

Charles Ivins is an ancestor of Anthony W. Ivins, who later became a member of the First Presidency of the LDS Church — an irony given Charles's role as a dissident.
