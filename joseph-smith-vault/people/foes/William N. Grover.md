# 👤 William N. Grover

**Tags:** #person/foe #era/nauvoo
**Occupation:** Captain, Warsaw Rifle Company; Lawyer
**Trial:** Defendant, *People v. Levi Williams et al.* (1845) — acquitted

---

## Summary

William N. Grover was the captain of the Warsaw Rifle Company and a lawyer who allegedly boasted about his role in the killing of Joseph Smith. His combination of legal training and militia command made him one of the more operationally significant figures among the Carthage defendants.

---

## Background

Grover's dual identity as both a lawyer and a militia captain is notable:
- As a **lawyer**, he understood the legal risks of participation in the mob and the likely inadequacy of prosecution
- As a **captain of the Warsaw Rifle Company**, he commanded one of the organized militia units that participated in the march on Carthage Jail

The fact that a legally trained man participated openly — and reportedly bragged about it afterward — reflects the culture of impunity that [[Thomas C. Sharp]]'s press had created in Hancock County.

---

## The Alleged Bragging

Reports circulated after the assassination that Grover openly boasted about his participation in the killing — confident that prosecution was either impossible or that acquittal was guaranteed in Hancock County.

Prosecution witness **Eliza Jane Graham** testified she saw Grover (along with Sharp and Davis) celebrating the murders at the Warsaw hotel.

---

## The 1845 Trial

Graham's testimony was undermined by **Ann Fleming** (Graham's aunt), who contradicted her niece's account in defense testimony. Combined with the broader structural problems of the prosecution, Grover was acquitted.

**Verdict:** Not guilty, May 30, 1845.

---

## Significance

Grover's reported bragging — if accurate — is the starkest illustration of the "culture of impunity" that the media atmosphere created by Sharp had produced. When participants feel free to boast about political assassination, the rule of law has effectively collapsed in that jurisdiction.
