# 🏛️ Liberty Jail

**Tags:** #theme/law #era/missouri
**Location:** Liberty, Clay County, Missouri
**Dates of Imprisonment:** December 1, 1838 – April 6, 1839
**Prisoners:** Joseph Smith, Hyrum Smith, Lyman Wight, Caleb Baldwin, Alexander McRae, Sidney Rigdon (briefly)

---

## Summary

Liberty Jail was a frontier stone prison in Clay County, Missouri, where Joseph Smith spent approximately four months following the surrender at Far West. It is one of the most theologically significant locations in LDS history — the "prison-temple" where Smith produced some of his most profound written revelations and epistles.

---

## Physical Conditions

The jail was a two-story stone structure with walls one foot thick:
- Lower level: dungeon, approximately 14 × 14 × 6.5 feet
- Prisoners were largely confined to the lower level through the winter of 1838–1839
- Food was reportedly tampered with, including at least one instance where human flesh was allegedly mixed into their meals (a claim in Mormon historical accounts)
- No adequate sanitation; bitterly cold Missouri winter

## Legal Status

The men were held on charges of **treason, murder, arson, and burglary** stemming from the 1838 Missouri War. General [[Austin A. King]] had presided over the Richmond Court of Inquiry that committed them.

Their continued imprisonment without trial was constitutionally problematic — the charges were politically motivated and the evidence was largely testimony from hostile witnesses. Eventually, Missouri authorities agreed to transport the prisoners to Boone County for a change of venue hearing — and "allowed" them to escape en route in April 1839.

---

## The Written Record

Despite the conditions, Smith produced an extraordinary body of writing from Liberty Jail:
- Long epistles to the church addressing the suffering of the Saints
- Doctrinal revelations now canonized as **Doctrine & Covenants sections 121, 122, and 123**
- Section 121 contains the famous passage: *"The heavens shall shake for your good, and Satan shall tremble and Zion shall rejoice upon the hills and flourish... How long can rolling waters remain impure?"*

---

## Theological Legacy

Liberty Jail became a "prison-temple" in LDS theological imagination — a site of suffering that produced prophetic revelation, parallel to biblical precedents (Joseph in Egypt, Paul in Rome). The site is now a visitor center maintained by the LDS church.
