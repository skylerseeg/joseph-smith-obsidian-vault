# ⚔️ The Missouri Mormon War (1838)

**Tags:** #theme/law #era/missouri
**Dates:** August – November 1838
**Location:** Caldwell, Daviess, Carroll Counties, Missouri
**Outcome:** Mormon expulsion from Missouri; Smith imprisoned at Liberty Jail

---

## Summary

The Missouri Mormon War of 1838 was the most violent and legally consequential conflict in Joseph Smith's career. It ended with the Mormon community expelled from Missouri under the "Extermination Order," Smith and other leaders imprisoned on charges of treason, and the church's entire Missouri settlement infrastructure destroyed.

---

## Background

Mormon settlement in Missouri had been contentious since 1831. The Saints were concentrated in Jackson County (1831–1833), Clay County (1833–1836), and ultimately in Caldwell County — which the Missouri legislature had designated specifically as a Mormon county in 1836, hoping to contain the religious community geographically.

By 1838, Caldwell County was filling beyond its boundaries, and Mormon settlers were moving into Daviess County — triggering new conflicts with existing settlers.

---

## Key Events

### August 1838 — Gallatin Election Day Battle
Mormon voters attempted to vote in Daviess County; they were attacked by anti-Mormon citizens at the polls. The resulting brawl triggered escalating militia mobilizations on both sides.

### September 1838 — Siege of De Witt
Anti-Mormon militia besieged the Mormon settlement at De Witt in Carroll County. Governor [[Lilburn W. Boggs]] refused to intervene. The Saints eventually evacuated.

### October 1838 — The "Danites" and Military Escalation
Mormon paramilitary units (the Danites) conducted raids on anti-Mormon settlements. Non-Mormon militias retaliated. The conflict spiraled beyond local control.

### October 25, 1838 — Battle of Crooked River
A Mormon militia unit attacked Captain [[Samuel Bogart]]'s Ray County militia, believing they held Mormon prisoners. Three Mormons and one militiaman were killed. This battle was used as justification for Governor Boggs's subsequent Extermination Order.

### October 27, 1838 — Extermination Order
Governor [[Lilburn W. Boggs]] issued **Executive Order 44**, declaring that "the Mormons must be treated as enemies, and must be exterminated or driven from the State."

### October 30, 1838 — Haun's Mill Massacre
Missouri militia attacked the small Mormon settlement at Haun's Mill. Seventeen Mormons were killed, including children. It remains the deadliest single act of anti-Mormon violence in American history.

### November 1, 1838 — Surrender of Far West
General [[Samuel D. Lucas]] surrounded Far West with the state militia. [[George M. Hinkle]] negotiated the surrender and delivered Smith and other leaders into custody.

General [[Alexander Doniphan]] **refused the execution order** from Lucas, saving Smith's life.

---

## Legal Aftermath

Smith and companions were taken to Richmond for the **Court of Inquiry** presided over by Judge [[Austin A. King]]:
- Charged with treason, murder, arson, and burglary
- Committed to [[Liberty Jail]] in Clay County

The men were eventually allowed to "escape" en route to a change of venue in April 1839.

---

## Second-Order Consequences

1. Produced Smith's "theodemocracy" worldview — only a government where religious leaders held military authority could protect the Saints
2. Directly led to the creation of the Nauvoo Legion and Council of Fifty
3. Generated the Missouri extradition attempts that plagued Smith through 1843
4. The Extermination Order remained unrepealed until **1976**
