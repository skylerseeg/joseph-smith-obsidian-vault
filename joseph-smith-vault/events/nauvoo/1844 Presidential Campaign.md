# 🏛️ The 1844 Presidential Campaign

**Tags:** #theme/politics #era/nauvoo
**Announced:** January 1844
**Ended:** June 27, 1844 (Smith's assassination)
**Platform Document:** *General Smith's Views of the Powers and Policy of the Government of the United States*

---

## Summary

Joseph Smith's 1844 presidential campaign was one of the most unusual candidacies in American history — a sitting mayor, militia general, and prophet running for president while under active extradition pressure from a neighboring state. It was simultaneously a serious organizational effort and a philosophical protest against the failure of American constitutional protections to safeguard religious minorities.

---

## Why Smith Ran

The campaign was a direct response to the **"Five Refusals"** — the systematic rejection of Mormon petitions by every major national political figure:
- [[Martin Van Buren]]: "Can do nothing for you"
- John C. Calhoun: Federal government lacks power
- Henry Clay: Non-committal
- Lewis Cass: Executive lacks power
- Richard M. Johnson: Non-committal

Smith's logic: if no existing candidate would protect religious liberty, put a candidate on the ballot who would make it the central issue.

---

## The Platform — *General Smith's Views*

Smith's platform was remarkably sophisticated and transcended simple religious advocacy:

**Economic policy:**
- Abolish imprisonment for debt
- Reduce Congressional pay (from $8/day to $2/day)
- Reduce the size of Congress by two-thirds

**Slavery:**
- Gradual abolition — compensate slaveholders from proceeds of public land sales
- "Pay every man a reasonable price for his slaves out of the surplus revenue"
- No immediate abolition; a transitional, compensatory approach

**Federal power:**
- Expand presidential power to protect citizens from state tyranny
- This was the direct anti-states'-rights plank — the lesson of Missouri applied nationally

**Prison reform:**
- Transform prisons into "seminaries of learning"
- Rehabilitative rather than punitive incarceration

**Oregon/Texas:**
- Annex Oregon; establish working relationship with Texas (where the [[Council of Fifty]] was simultaneously negotiating Mormon settlement)

## The Campaign Organization

The [[Council of Fifty]] functioned as Smith's campaign apparatus:

| Role | Person |
|---|---|
| Political Clerk / Campaign Manager | [[W. W. Phelps]] |
| Distribution of platform documents | Apostolic missionaries deployed as campaigners |
| National correspondence | [[W. W. Phelps]], [[Willard Richards]] |
| Vice Presidential first choice | James A. Bennet (declined — not U.S.-born) |

Thousands of copies of *General Smith's Views* were distributed across the country. Missionary electioneers were deployed to every state.

---

## The Electoral Strategy

Smith's strategy exploited the tight margin of antebellum politics:
- The 1844 election was expected to be close between Clay (Whig) and Polk (Democrat)
- The Mormon community in Illinois, Indiana, and other Midwestern states was large enough to swing close states
- By running, Smith could threaten both parties' calculations and force concessions on religious liberty

The strategy was never tested — Smith was killed five months before election day.

---

## The Campaign's Historical Significance

The 1844 campaign is studied as:
1. The first serious presidential run by a religious leader of a minority sect
2. One of the earliest national platforms to propose compensated emancipation
3. A genuine expression of the "theodemocracy" concept — religious authority seeking temporal political power
4. A political protest that exposed constitutional failures regarding minority religious rights
