# ⚖️ 1845 Carthage Conspiracy Trial

**Case Name:** *People v. Levi Williams et al.*
**Date:** May 1845
**Location:** Carthage, Hancock County, Illinois
**Verdict:** Not guilty — all defendants — May 30, 1845
**Tags:** #theme/law #era/nauvoo

---

## Background

Joseph Smith and his brother [[Hyrum Smith]] were assassinated on June 27, 1844, at Carthage Jail. The attack was carried out by a mob of approximately 100–200 men with blackened faces.

In October 1844, a grand jury indicted nine men. Four fled the jurisdiction:
- John Wills
- William Voras
- Gallaher (first name unknown)
- Allen (first name unknown)

The remaining five were brought to trial in May 1845.

---

## The Courtroom

- **Presiding Judge:** Richard M. Young
- **Setting:** Courthouse filled with armed partisans
- **Atmosphere:** The prosecution faced a structural impossibility — [[Thomas C. Sharp]]'s years of editorials had made an impartial jury in Hancock County virtually impossible

## The Defendants

| Defendant | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| [[Thomas C. Sharp]] | Editor, *Warsaw Signal* | Primary organizer; Anti-Mormon Political Party |
| [[Levi Williams]] | Baptist minister; Col., 59th Militia | Allegedly commanded the charge on the jail |
| [[Mark Aldrich]] | Land developer; militia battalion commander | Personal land grievances against the Saints |
| [[Jacob C. Davis]] | State Senator; Warsaw Cadets commander | Turned against Mormons after they denied him Congressional support in 1844 |
| [[William N. Grover]] | Captain, Warsaw Rifle Co.; Lawyer | Reportedly bragged about the killing |

## Legal Teams

**Prosecution (State of Illinois)**
- **[[Josiah Lamborn]]** — Illinois Attorney General
- Critical weakness: Lamborn essentially disavowed his own star witnesses during closing arguments

**Defense ("Dream Team")**
- **[[Orville H. Browning]]** — Prominent Whig attorney (had defended Smith in 1843 extradition)
- **William A. Richardson** — Co-counsel

## Key Witnesses

| Witness | Side | Testimony |
|---|---|---|
| William M. Daniels | Prosecution | Claimed a "pillar of light" prevented the mob from severing Smith's head after death |
| Eliza Jane Graham | Prosecution | Testified Sharp, Grover, and Davis celebrated the murders at the Warsaw hotel |
| Benjamin Brackenbury | Prosecution | Corroborated Graham's hotel account |
| John Peyton | Prosecution | Refused to state defendants intended to kill |
| Franklin A. Worrell | Prosecution | Committed "obvious perjury" — denied seeing any defendant at the jail |
| Ann Fleming | Defense | Graham's aunt; contradicted Graham's testimony |

### The Eyewitness Problem

[[John Taylor]] (who survived the attack) and other Latter-day Saint eyewitnesses **refused to participate** in what they viewed as a "rigged proceeding." This denial of the prosecution's most credible witnesses was fatal to the case.

---

## Structural Analysis: Why Acquittal Was Inevitable

1. **Jury pool contamination** — Sharp's *Warsaw Signal* had framed the killers as patriots. No impartial juror could be found locally.
2. **Witness intimidation and refusal** — Mormon eyewitnesses boycotted. Non-Mormon witnesses who were present committed perjury or gave evasive testimony.
3. **Prosecution self-sabotage** — Lamborn disavowed his own witnesses.
4. **Armed gallery** — The courtroom itself contained armed anti-Mormon partisans.

---

## Aftermath & Consequences

The not-guilty verdict on May 30, 1845 had a singular, world-historical consequence:

> It confirmed for the Saints that justice under the laws of the United States was impossible.

This belief directly accelerated the decision to abandon Nauvoo and begin the exodus to the Great Basin, leading to the founding of Salt Lake City in 1847 under [[Brigham Young]].
