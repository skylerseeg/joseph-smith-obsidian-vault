# 📖 Book of Mormon — Publication (1830)

**Tags:** #theme/scriptures #era/new-york
**Published:** March 26, 1830
**Publisher:** E. B. Grandin, Palmyra, New York
**Print run:** 5,000 copies
**Cost:** $3,000 (funded by [[Martin Harris]]'s farm mortgage)

---

## Summary

The publication of the Book of Mormon on March 26, 1830, was the foundational public act of Joseph Smith's prophetic career — the physical artifact that converted private visionary experience into a public religious claim. Everything that followed — the legal proceedings, the media coverage, the social network — was a response to this document's existence.

---

## The Translation Process

Smith reported translating the Book of Mormon from golden plates using two instruments:
- The **Urim and Thummim** (described as two transparent stones in silver bows, attached to a breastplate)
- A **seer stone** (a brown stone placed in a hat, into which Smith buried his face to read illuminated words)

**Primary scribe:** [[Oliver Cowdery]] (April–June 1829)
**Earlier scribes:** [[Emma Hale Smith]], Martin Harris (who was responsible for the lost 116 pages)
**Location:** Harmony, Pennsylvania (early stages); Fayette, New York (completion, at Whitmer farm)

The translation proceeded at a pace of approximately 7–8 pages per day during the most productive period.

---

## The Witnesses

### Three Witnesses (June 28, 1829)
[[Oliver Cowdery]], [[David Whitmer]], [[Martin Harris]] — reported seeing the plates shown by an angel. Signed the formal "Testimony of Three Witnesses" included in the Book of Mormon.

All three were later excommunicated from the church. None recanted their testimony.

### Eight Witnesses (June 1829)
A separate group — Christian Whitmer, Jacob Whitmer, Peter Whitmer Jr., John Whitmer, Hiram Page, Joseph Smith Sr., Hyrum Smith, Samuel H. Smith — reported physically handling the plates. Also signed a formal testimony.

---

## Content Overview

The Book of Mormon presents itself as a record of ancient inhabitants of the Americas, covering approximately 600 BC to AD 421. Key theological contributions:
- Presents Christ as appearing to ancient Americans after his resurrection
- Contains extensive doctrinal discussions parallel to New Testament themes
- Addresses 19th-century theological controversies (infant baptism, grace vs. works)
- Ends with a promise (Moroni 10:4) that readers who pray sincerely will receive divine confirmation of its truth

---

## The Abner Cole Episode

[[Abner Cole]] was printing his *Palmyra Reflector* in Grandin's print shop part-time, giving him access to the Book of Mormon manuscript before publication. He began publishing excerpts without permission. Smith and his brother Hyrum confronted Cole and legally forced him to stop — but not before Cole had begun the pattern of satirical anti-Mormon journalism that would persist for decades.

---

## Commercial Performance

The initial 5,000-copy print run sold slowly at first. By the end of 1830, however, missionary work was distributing the book across multiple states. By 1840, it had been printed in England and was circulating internationally.

---

## Critical Response

| Critic | Work | Core Argument |
|---|---|---|
| Alexander Campbell | *Delusions* (1832) | Smith was sole author; the book addresses every 19th-century theological controversy |
| [[Eber D. Howe]] | *Mormonism Unvailed* (1834) | Book plagiarized from Solomon Spaulding's unpublished manuscript |
| [[Abner Cole]] | *Palmyra Reflector* (1830–1831) | Mockery; published unauthorized excerpts |

The Spaulding Theory dominated critical scholarship until 1884, when Spaulding's actual manuscript was discovered and shown to bear minimal resemblance to the Book of Mormon.
