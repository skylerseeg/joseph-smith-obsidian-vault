## Footnotes

**[1]** In 1842, the Louisiana state treasurer recommended “the passage of a law laying a tax on strangers coming into the state, for the support of the hospitals and other charitable institutions to which strangers have access.” Apostle John Taylor also stated in a meeting of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles that, according to newspaper reports, “the Brittish Parliament had taken prohibitory measures agai[n]st Emigration.” (“Report of the State Treasurer,” Baton Rouge [LA] Gazette, 5 Feb. 1842, [2]; Quorum of the Twelve Apostles, Minutes, 11 May 1843.)
**[2]** In a letter published in the November 1842 issue of the Millennial Star, Ward noted reports that may have been similar to the reports he referred to here. According to Ward, the migration of Saints from England to Nauvoo seemed to “rouse the ire” of Liverpool residents, leading to local newspaper reports “teem[ing] with inuendoes, cautions, histories, &c. of the Mormon delusion.” (Thomas Ward, “To the Saints in Europe,” Millennial Star, Nov. 1842, 3:125.)
