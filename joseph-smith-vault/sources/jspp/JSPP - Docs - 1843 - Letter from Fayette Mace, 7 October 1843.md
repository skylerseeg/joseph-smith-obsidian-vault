**[1]** This likely referred to Latter-day Saint Perrigrine Sessions. Sessions settled in Nauvoo, Illinois, returning to his home state of Maine as a missionary in 1839 or 1840. In April 1843, he again left Nauvoo to preach in Maine, spending considerable time in the area near Farmington between June 1843 and May 1844. (Sessions, Reminiscences and Diaries, vol. 1, pp. [17]–[36].)
**[2]** This likely referred to Latter-day Saints Henry Harriman and Thomas Butterfield. In 1842, Harriman and Butterfield were called to “go on a mission to the eastern States to preach the Gospel.” Sometime before fall 1843, the two men preached in Farmington, Maine, the town in which Butterfield was born and near where he lived until at least 1840. While in Farmington, Harriman and Thomas Butterfield baptized Hannah and Martha Butterfield, who were possibly Thomas’s relatives. (Harriman, Autobiography, 9–10; Farmington, ME, Births Prior to 1850, 34, microfilm 855,272, U.S. and Canada Record Collection, FHL; 1840 U.S. Census, Wilton, Franklin Co., ME, 146; Wright, Journal, 25 May 1844; Nauvoo, IL, Recorder, Marriage Record, 24.)
