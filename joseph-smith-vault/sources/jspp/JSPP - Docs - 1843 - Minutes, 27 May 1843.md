The main issue seems to have been Hess loaning money from church members to Adams, something that Hess denied. After “disscussing the subject fully,” the council decided to drop the matter and to have Hess pay sixteen dollars as rent for the church building the branch was using. The same meeting also considered charges that James B. Nicholson made against Winchester for disparaging the character of his mother, Eliza Nicholson. When Hyrum Smith and William Law reorganized the Philadelphia branch in October 1842, Smith counseled the branch to forget “all former dificulties” and never speak of them again. **[3]** Letter from Sybella McMinn Armstrong, 1 May 1843. According to William Clayton, when JS received Armstrong's letter on 22 May, he gave it to Richards and said, “The Twelve ought to silence Winchester.” (Clayton, Journal, 22 May 1843.)

**[5]** According to Armstrong, Winchester told people that she “was kno[w]n to all the Capta[i]ns on the Ohio and Missippie [Mississippi] Rivers.” (Letter from Sybella McMinn Armstrong, 1 May 1843.)
