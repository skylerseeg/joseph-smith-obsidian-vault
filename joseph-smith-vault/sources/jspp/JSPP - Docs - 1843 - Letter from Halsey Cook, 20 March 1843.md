## Footnotes

**[1]** Cook could have been referring here to either the Book of Mormon or the Doctrine and Covenants, a collection of JS’s revelations.
**[2]** Based on the 1842 Nauvoo census, one scholar estimates the city’s population to have been around 4,000 people in that year. In 1845 the Times and Seasons reprinted an article from the Saint Louis Evening Gazette that counted the city’s population at 11,057, with “a third more” outside the city limits. Church members also lived in many other locations in the United States and in Great Britain, making it difficult to accurately estimate membership in 1843. It is also unclear how many missionaries were preaching at that time. (Nauvoo Stake, Ward Census, 1842, CHL; Black, “How Large Was the Population of Nauvoo?,” 92; “Mobocracy,” Times and Seasons, 15 Nov. 1845, 6:1031.)
**[3]** A letter written by Willard Richards on JS’s behalf in 1843 described Nauvoo as “located on the east bench of the Mississippi River, in a fruitful county, best of soil all around, as healthy as any of the western country.” (Letter to John McKee, 28 Mar. 1843.)
