Thompson handwriting begins.This may certify that for value received in Stereotyp[e] plates of the Book of Mormon,1Stereotyping was a printing innovation that grew in popularity in the United States during the 1820s. Traditionally, printers set moveable type for a certain number of pages, printed those pages, and then reused that same type in printing subsequent pages. If an author wanted to print additional copies after a book was printed, the entire typesetting process had to happen again. Stereotyping—forming plaster molds of typeset pages and pouring melted metal in the mold—resulted in permanent page plates. These plates were an expensive investment but one that saved much time when reprinting a book.
