## Footnotes

**[1]** Acting as a local agent for the New York Land Company, Kilbourne began purchasing land in Lee County, Iowa Territory, during the late 1830s; he also owned and operated a general store in Montrose, Lee County. Difficulties between him and JS appear to have developed in 1841. In mid-September, JS and John C. Bennett, respectively lieutenant general and major general of the Nauvoo Legion, were invited to attend a military parade of the Lee County militia in Montrose. At that gathering, Kilbourne apparently read aloud a proclamation insisting that Smith had no right to inspect the militia. Later that month Kilbourne wrote a series of letters to the Burlington Hawkeye and Patriot (later reprinted in the Warsaw Signal) in which he accused Latter-day Saint settlers of orchestrating thirteen robberies in Montrose. (“Mason-Remey and the Kilbourne Collections,” 310–311; David W. Kilbourne, Statement, Keokuk, IA, 22 Sept. 1853, microfilm 960,050, item 3, U.S. and Canada Record Collection, FHL; JS History, vol.
