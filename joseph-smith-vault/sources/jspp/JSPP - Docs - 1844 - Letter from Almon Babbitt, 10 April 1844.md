## Footnotes

**[1]** On 11 March 1844, the Council of Fifty received JS as its “standing chairman.” Thereafter he was often referred to by his official council position of “chairman” but was also often referred to by his church office of “President” or by some abbreviation thereof. For example, William Clayton’s record of the 11 April 1844 meeting begins by noting that “Prest. J. Smith [was] in the chair.” (Council of Fifty, “Record,” 11 Mar. and 11 Apr. 1844.)
