In January 1842, Coe was seeking a house to rent in Kirtland in which he could live and board students of a local seminary. (Letter from Reuben McBride, 3 Jan. 1842.) Coe agreed to pay ninety dollars per year in rent and half of the tax on the land. In his letter, McBride informed JS that Coe paid less than half of the rent in 1842 and, despite promising to settle the account, did not pay the full rent in 1843 either. McBride also stated that Coe had not paid his portion of the taxes on the land and that without timely payment, McBride would not be able to pay the forty-five dollars of taxes owed to the county. A reliable source had informed McBride that Coe would not pay the rent and instead planned to write to JS to try to get ownership of the farm. McBride suspected Coe would intentionally not pay his portion of the taxes owed on the property so that the government would seize the farm, thereby allowing Coe to purchase it at auction for less money.
