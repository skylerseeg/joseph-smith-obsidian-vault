## Footnotes

**[]** Thomas Ward handwriting begins.
**[1]** In early April 1841, Fielding was appointed to be the “agent of the church, to superintend the fitting out of the Saints from Liverpool to America.” He was to act “under the instructions of P[arley] P. Pratt.” The decision came in response to several British members having been “robbed and cheated out of nearly all they had” by trying to arrange their own emigration to the United States. (Woodruff, Journal, 2 Apr. 1841; Quorum of the Twelve Apostles, Minutes, 2 Apr. 1841; Brigham Young et al., “An Epistle of the Twelve,” Millennial Star, Apr. 1841, 1:311.)
**[2]** At New Orleans, Fielding’s company arranged for passage to St. Louis on a steamboat named the General Pratt. (Palmer, Reminiscences, 66.)
**[3]** Snider, who was in England, was the secretary of the Nauvoo House Association and signed the stock certificates for the association. Snider had been appointed by the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles to serve a mission to England to collect funds for the Nauvoo temple. (Nauvoo House Association, Stock Certificate, 11 Feb. 1841, CHL; Brigham Young et al., “An Epistle of the Twelve,” Times and Seasons, 1 Apr. 1842, 3:736–737; JS, Journal, 23 Jan. 1843.)
