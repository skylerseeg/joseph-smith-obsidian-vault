Compared to the earlier agreements, the arrangement with Pinney, Phillips, and their agents gave them significant autonomy, more time to circulate the society’s notes, and a larger amount of capital from which to base their loans. The Kirtland Safety Society committed to provide $40,000 in the society’s notes to Pinney and Phillips over the next four years. The independence given to Pinney and Phillips suggests a different role than agents had previously played in the circulation of the society’s notes. Additionally, they were required to mark the Kirtland Safety Society notes given to them with their names and to deposit money with the society to redeem those notes.
