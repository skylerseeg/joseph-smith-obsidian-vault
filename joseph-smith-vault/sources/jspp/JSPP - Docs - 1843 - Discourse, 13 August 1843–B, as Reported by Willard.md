**[3]** Grog shop and grocery were colloquial terms for unlicensed establishments that sold spirits, or alcoholic beverages, by the glass; the term beer barrel was slang for a drunkard. Nauvoo city law prohibited the sale of spirituous liquors in small quantities (that is, by the glass) unless recommended by a physician. For a short period, the Nauvoo City Council licensed sellers to dispense some vinous liquors and beer, but the council repealed those ordinances in May 1842. Though the public consumption of alcohol was discouraged, neither production nor home consumption was prohibited by law. On 7 March 1843, for example, Theodore Turley was permitted to open a brewery at the corner of Hyde and Water streets.
