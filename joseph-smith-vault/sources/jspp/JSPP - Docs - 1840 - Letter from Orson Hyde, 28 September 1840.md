Over the preceding decades, Muhammad (Mehmet) Ali Pasha of Egypt had been continually expanding his control of Ottoman territory through successive military campaigns. By 1840 the Ottoman Empire was on the brink of total defeat by Muhammad Ali, and on 15 July 1840, Britain, Russia, Prussia, and the Austrian Empire signed a treaty called the Convention of London. This treaty offered support to the Ottoman Empire provided that Muhammad Ali accept permanent control of Egypt, Sudan, and the Eyalet of Acre and that these territories remain officially part of the Ottoman Empire. Disagreement within the European nations about how to handle the situation, in addition to the threat of France entering the conflict in support of Muhammad Ali, resulted in the tensions Hyde described.
