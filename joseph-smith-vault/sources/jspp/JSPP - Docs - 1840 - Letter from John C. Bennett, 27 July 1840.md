## Footnotes

**[1]** Although JS responded to Bennett on 8 August 1840, saying, “I have not the pleasure of your acquaintance,” Bennett had apparently met JS and Rigdon when William E. McLellin introduced him to them on 12 and 13 January 1832. (Letter to John C. Bennett, 8 Aug. 1840; McLellin, Journal, 12–13 Jan. 1832, 13.)
**[2]** The United States Circuit Court for the District of Illinois was moved to Springfield in the late 1830s, where court was held twice each year, in June and December. Federal district judge Nathaniel Pope presided over the June 1839 court, and Bennett served on the jury. (History of Sangamon County, Illinois, 84–85; Meese, “Nathaniel Pope,” 19, 21.)
**[3]** In April 1839, church leaders chose the Commerce area as the Saints’ new gathering place, and a general conference of the church approved the decision in October 1839. (Minutes, 24 Apr. 1839; Minutes and Discourses, 5–7 Oct. 1839.)
