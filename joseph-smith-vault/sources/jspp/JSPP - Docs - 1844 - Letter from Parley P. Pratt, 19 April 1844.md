## Footnotes

**[1]** The boat was probably the James Madison, reportedly the first steamer to run regularly between Buffalo and Chicago. The James Madison had been making the trip since at least 1837. (Hilton, Lake Michigan Passenger Steamers, 28–29; Notice, Milwaukie Sentinel [Milwaukee], 9 Nov. 1844, [3].)
**[2]** Pratt’s estimate may have been a little conservative. Although the voyage was sometimes faster, the average passage from Chicago to Buffalo alone took six days at the time. (Hilton, Lake Michigan Passenger Steamers, 32.)
**[3]** The intended Illinois and Michigan Canal would connect the Great Lakes with the Illinois River and effectively link New York and the northeastern United States with the Mississippi River and all its navigable tributaries. The greatly anticipated canal was widely envisioned as a continental thoroughfare of major geographic and economic importance. Although construction of the ninety-six-mile canal, connecting Lake Michigan with the Illinois River from Chacago to LaSalle, began in 1836, the canal was not completed until 1848. (Ranney and Harris, Prairie Passage, 37–43.)
