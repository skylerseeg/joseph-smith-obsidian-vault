## Footnotes

**[1]** According to the minutes of the founding of the high council, those who drew odd lots—numbers 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, and 11—spoke for the church or the accuser, while those who drew even lots—numbers 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, and 12—spoke for the accused. As shown at the end of these minutes, the high council sometimes ordered itself at the end of a council for its next meeting. That may have been done here, as neither Jared Carter nor Martin Harris was present to draw lots. The numbering system here is unique in that scribes typically did not assign a numbering arrangement for the list of high counselors. It seems the scribe must have ordered the lists numerically according to the side for which the counselors were to speak, rather than by even and odd numbers. (Minutes, 17 Feb. 1834; Revised Minutes, 18–19 Feb. 1834 [D&C 102:12–18].)
