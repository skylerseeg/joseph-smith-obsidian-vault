# ✨ First Vision Accounts

**Tags:** #theme/scriptures #era/new-york
**Core Event (claimed):** Spring 1820, Sacred Grove, near Palmyra, New York

---

## Summary

The First Vision is Joseph Smith's foundational claim — the divine encounter in which he reported seeing God the Father and Jesus Christ as separate, embodied beings who told him not to join any existing church. Multiple accounts of this event exist, recorded across Smith's career by different scribes and for different audiences. The variation between accounts is one of the most studied questions in Mormon historiography.

---

## The Core Narrative

Smith reported that in the spring of 1820, troubled by religious competition among denominations, he went to a grove of trees near his family's farm in Palmyra, New York, to pray. He was guided by James 1:5: *"If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God."*

**What he reported seeing:**
- A pillar of light descending
- Two personages — identified as God the Father and Jesus Christ
- A message: do not join any existing church, for "they are all corrupt"
- (In later accounts): the promise that his sins were forgiven and that a restoration of the true church would follow

## The Major Accounts

| Account | Year | Scribe/Publisher | Key Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1832 Account | 1832 | Joseph Smith (own hand) | Earliest; most personal; emphasizes forgiveness of sins; one personage unclear |
| 1835 Account | 1835 | Warren Parrish | Told to Robert Matthews; includes angels |
| 1838 Account | 1838 | Scribes (official history) | The "canonical" version; two personages clearly named |
| 1842 Account | 1842 | Joseph Smith | Published in the *Wentworth Letter*; widely distributed |

### The [[David Nye White]] Account (1843)
White's August 1843 interview recorded Smith's spoken description of the First Vision for a secular newspaper audience:
- "A glorious personage in the light, and then another personage"
- Told not to join any existing church "because they are all corrupt"
- Described the preceding denominational confusion as the trigger

### The [[Orson Pratt]] Printed Account (1840)
Published in Edinburgh, Scotland — the **first printed version** of the First Vision narrative. Published as part of *A Interesting Account of Several Remarkable Visions*.

### The Orson Hyde German Account (1842)
First German-language account of Smith's visions — reflecting the international missionary reach of the church by the early 1840s.

## Historiographical Debate

### The "Lateness" Question
Critics note that the 1832 account (the earliest) was not publicly known until the 20th century, and the "canonical" 1838 account was not published until 1842. If the First Vision was so foundational, why does it appear to have been unknown or unemphasized in early church publications?

**LDS response:** The 1832 account was a private journal entry; public proclamation of the vision developed as the church matured and needed to articulate its founding claims.

### The "Variation" Question
The accounts vary in the number of personages seen, the role of angels, the precise message, and the emphasis on personal forgiveness vs. restoration commission.

**LDS response:** Multiple accounts of any remembered experience naturally emphasize different aspects for different audiences and purposes.

**Critical response:** The variations suggest development and embellishment over time, not consistent memory of a single event.

### The Historical Silence Question
No contemporary accounts (non-Mormon newspaper reports, neighbor affidavits) from 1820 mention a revival that Smith says triggered his inquiry.

**LDS response:** The revival may have been small and local; absence of evidence is not evidence of absence.
