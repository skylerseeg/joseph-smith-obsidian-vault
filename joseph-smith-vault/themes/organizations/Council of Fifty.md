# 🏛️ Council of Fifty

**Tags:** #theme/politics #theme/organizations #era/nauvoo
**Organized:** March 1844
**Location:** Nauvoo, Illinois
**Also Known As:** "The Kingdom of God and His Laws," "The General Council"

---

## Summary

The Council of Fifty was the most radical political institution Joseph Smith created — a secret governing body tasked with preparing for the literal establishment of a theocratic "Kingdom of God" on earth. Operating parallel to the Nauvoo city government, it conducted quasi-diplomatic relations with foreign nations and coordinated Smith's 1844 presidential campaign.

---

## Organization

- **Organized:** March 1844 — the final three months of Smith's life
- **Membership:** Approximately 50 men, drawn from the church's senior leadership
- **Clerk:** [[William Clayton]] — who kept the minutes in cipher
- **Recorder:** [[Willard Richards]]
- **Nature:** Secret — members swore oaths of confidentiality

## Purposes and Activities

### 1. Preparing the Political Kingdom
Smith taught that before Christ's return, a literal political "Kingdom of God" must be established. The Council of Fifty was that government in embryo — with Smith as its "King" (though this title was rarely used openly).

### 2. The 1844 Presidential Campaign
The Council served as Smith's de facto campaign organization:
- Coordinated the distribution of *General Smith's Views* nationwide
- Sent electioneering missionaries across the country
- Drafted correspondence to [[Henry Clay]], [[John C. Calhoun]], and other candidates

### 3. Quasi-Diplomatic Activity
Recognizing that American constitutional protection had failed the Saints (per the Missouri experience), the Council explored alternatives:
- Sent emissaries to **Sam Houston** regarding Mormon settlement in the Republic of Texas
- Explored possibilities in California, Oregon, and Vancouver Island
- Drafted a memorial to Congress requesting a territorial grant in the West

---

## Documentary Legacy

The Council of Fifty minutes — kept by [[William Clayton]] in cipher — were sealed for over 150 years. They were finally published as part of the **Joseph Smith Papers Project** in 2016, providing the first comprehensive documentary account of the Council's activities.

---

## Significance

The Council of Fifty represents the fullest expression of Smith's "theodemocracy" concept — a political order where divine law superseded human law, administered by religious leaders. Its existence confirmed every fear critics like [[Thomas C. Sharp]] and [[Thomas Ford]] had expressed about Smith's theocratic ambitions.

From the outside, it looked exactly like what they said it was: a secret government planning to replace the United States.
