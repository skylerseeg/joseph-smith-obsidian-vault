# ⚔️ Nauvoo Legion

**Tags:** #theme/organizations #theme/politics #era/nauvoo
**Organized:** February 1841
**Authorized by:** Illinois state legislature (as part of Nauvoo Charter)
**Commander:** Joseph Smith (Lieutenant General)
**Peak strength:** ~3,000–5,000 men

---

## Summary

The Nauvoo Legion was the most visible and alarming symbol of Mormon power in Illinois — a quasi-state military force commanded by the prophet-mayor himself, equipped with artillery and infantry, drilling regularly in the streets of Nauvoo. It was simultaneously a legitimate Illinois state militia and a personal army for a theocratic leader, a combination that made it constitutionally defensible and politically terrifying to non-Mormon neighbors.

---

## Legal Foundation

The **Nauvoo Charter** (drafted primarily by [[John C. Bennett]], granted by the Illinois legislature in December 1840) authorized the city to establish its own militia — the Nauvoo Legion — as a unit of the Illinois state militia.

This meant:
- The Legion was technically a **state military unit**, subject to the Governor's call
- But it was commanded by Smith as Lieutenant General — a rank that made him technically the senior militia officer in the state (Illinois had no officer of equal rank at the time)
- The charter's habeas corpus provisions allowed Nauvoo courts to release Legion members from state process

---

## The Semi-Annual Musters

The Legion held large public musters — military reviews attended by thousands. These events served multiple purposes:
- Military training and readiness
- Public display of Mormon military power (deterrent)
- Community pageantry and social cohesion
- Political demonstration of the scale of Mormon organization

When [[Josiah Quincy]] visited Nauvoo in May 1844, he noted the Legion's 3,000 men as one of the most striking aspects of Smith's power.

---

## Why It Alarmed Non-Mormons

To non-Mormon observers in Illinois:
- A religious leader commanding a private army of thousands
- Artillery and infantry drilling on a regular schedule
- The same leader who was also mayor, judge, and head of a secret political council
- A community that voted as a bloc and directed its commerce internally

[[Thomas C. Sharp]]'s *Warsaw Signal* hammered on the Legion constantly — framing it as proof that Smith was building a military despotism rather than a religious community.

The Legion's existence directly informed Governor [[Thomas Ford]]'s anxiety about the *Nauvoo Expositor* crisis — he feared that if he didn't act against Smith, the Legion might be used to resist state authority.

---

## The Irony

The Nauvoo Legion was the Saints' response to having been defenseless in Missouri — no organized military force to resist the state militia in 1838. The lesson they drew was that survival required armed preparation.

The Legion's creation confirmed every fear their Illinois neighbors had about Mormon theocratic ambition, accelerating the crisis that made Carthage inevitable.

---

## After the Martyrdom

The Illinois state legislature revoked the Nauvoo Charter in January 1845 — effectively dissolving the Legion's legal basis. The Legion continued to exist informally until the Saints left Nauvoo in 1846.
