# 🏛️ Politics MOC — Smith's National Political Network

---

## Core Thesis

Smith's presidential campaign was a sophisticated exercise in **theological protest** — not a serious bid for power, but a systematic exposure of how "states' rights" doctrine failed to protect religious minorities.

> By corresponding with Calhoun and Clay, Smith was highlighting the structural failure of the American constitutional system to protect a cohesive, armed, propertied religious community.

---

## The Presidential Campaign (1844)

- **Announcement:** January 1844
- **Platform:** "General Smith's Views of the Powers and Policy of the Government of the United States"
- **Political Clerk / Campaign Manager:** [[W. W. Phelps]]
- **Running mate first choice:** James A. Bennet (New York educator; declined — not U.S.-born)
- **Outcome:** Smith was assassinated June 27, 1844, before the election

**Platform highlights:**
- Abolish imprisonment for debt
- Reduce Congressional pay
- Gradual abolition of slavery via government compensation to slaveholders
- Expand federal power to protect citizens from state tyranny

## The Stephen A. Douglas Trajectory

Douglas is the paradigmatic example of political expediency overriding personal alliance:

1. "Immediately befriended" Smith for the Mormon vote when Smith arrived in Illinois (1838)
2. Served as a helpful early ally in Illinois politics
3. As the "Mormon problem" became a liability in the broader Illinois electorate, distanced himself
4. Eventually attacked the Church as a "disgusting ulcer" — a complete reversal

This pattern is described in [[The Prophetic Circle]] as illustrating "the causal link between political expediency and social betrayal."

## The Council of Fifty

Smith's most radical political structure. Organized in Nauvoo in 1843–1844.

**Purpose:** Prepare for a literal political "Kingdom of God" — a shadow cabinet capable of international diplomacy.

**Known activities:**
- Sent emissaries to Sam Houston re: Texas settlement
- Explored California/Oregon options
- Functioned as Smith's secret governing body parallel to the city council

**Key clerks:** [[Willard Richards]], [[William Clayton]]

## The "Theodemocracy" Concept

After the Missouri betrayal (1838–1839), Smith developed the concept of "theodemocracy" — a state where religious leaders held ultimate civil and military authority. This directly informed:
- The extraordinary powers of the Nauvoo Charter
- The Nauvoo Legion (Smith as Lt. General)
- The Council of Fifty
- The declaration of candidacy for President

## Notable Observers

**Josiah Quincy** (Mayor of Boston) visited Nauvoo with Charles Francis Adams (son of John Quincy Adams) just 45 days before Smith's death. Quincy wrote one of the most nuanced secular accounts of Smith — calling him a "rare human being" while questioning his theology.
