# ⚖️ Legal MOC — Joseph Smith and the American Judicial System

> Joseph Smith was involved in approximately **220 legal cases** as plaintiff, defendant, witness, or judge. This hub maps the major proceedings.

---

## Legal Arc Overview

Smith's legal journey evolved from petty misdemeanors to capital crimes against the state:

```
1826 → "Disorderly Person" (glasslooker)
1830 → "Disorderly Person" (South Bainbridge)
1834 → Death threat complaint vs. Hurlbut
1837 → Illegal banking charges (Ohio)
1838 → Treason + Murder (Missouri) → Liberty Jail
1843 → Extradition hearings (Boggs assassination attempt)
1844 → Riot → Treason → Assassination at Carthage
1845 → People v. Levi Williams (acquittal)
```

## Key Cases

### 1826 — The Glasslooker Trial
- **Location:** South Bainbridge, New York
- **Charge:** "Disorderly person" — using a seer stone to defraud
- **Complainant:** Peter Bridgeman (nephew of [[Josiah Stowell]])
- **Outcome:** Disputed — either discharged or "took leg bail"
- **Significance:** First documented legal proceeding; establishes folk magic context

### 1834 — Hurlbut Threat Case
- **Location:** Painesville, Ohio
- **Charge:** Smith filed against [[Philastus Hurlbut]] for threatening his life
- **Smith testified:** Three hours about his fears
- **Defense:** Benjamin Bissell
- **Outcome:** Hurlbut restrained

### 1838–1839 — The Missouri Proceedings
- **Charges:** Treason, murder, arson, burglary
- **Context:** Surrender of Far West; General [[Samuel D. Lucas]] ordered execution
- **Hero:** [[Alexander Doniphan]] refused the execution order
- **Judge:** [[Austin A. King]] — Richmond Criminal Court of Inquiry
- **Result:** Committed to Liberty Jail; Smith "escaped" to Illinois

### 1843 — Boggs Extradition Hearing
- **Location:** Springfield, Illinois
- **Court:** Federal District Court
- **Judge:** [[Nathaniel Pope]]
- **Defense:** [[Justin Butterfield]] (U.S. Attorney) and [[Orville H. Browning]]
- **Key argument:** Missouri cannot extradite for a crime committed when Smith was not in that state
- **Outcome:** Smith freed

### 1844 — The Final Legal Crisis
- **Trigger:** Destruction of the *Nauvoo Expositor*
- **Initial charge:** Riot (before Constable William Bettisworth)
- **Escalated to:** Treason (under Illinois Governor [[Thomas Ford]])
- **Outcome:** Smith surrendered to Carthage; assassinated June 27, 1844
