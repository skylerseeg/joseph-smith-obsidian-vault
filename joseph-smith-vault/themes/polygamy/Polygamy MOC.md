# 💍 Polygamy MOC — Plural Marriage in Joseph Smith's Life

> Historians estimate Smith was sealed to between **30 and 40 women**, with some lists reaching 49. All sealings were religious; none were recognized under civil law.

---

## Categories of Relationship

| Category | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Legal Marriage | Only civil union, recognized by state law | [[Emma Hale Smith]] |
| Plural Marriages (Single Women) | New sealings to unmarried women | [[Louisa Beaman]], Partridge sisters |
| Polyandrous Sealings | Sealings to women with living legal husbands | [[Zina Diantha Huntington]], [[Mary Elizabeth Rollins]] |
| Dynastic Sealings | Linking prominent families eternally | [[Helen Mar Kimball]] (daughter of [[Heber C. Kimball]]) |

### Polyandrous Sealings Note
These were often described as "eternity-only" sealings that did not necessarily imply sexual cohabitation. The evidence column in the table below tracks the level of documented sexual evidence for each relationship.

---

## The First Plural Marriage: Fanny Alger (~1833–1836)

- Occurred in Kirtland, Ohio
- Generally recognized as Smith's first plural wife
- Discovered in a barn by [[Oliver Cowdery]], who later described it privately as a "dirty, nasty, filthy affair"
- Nothing is known of the conversations between Joseph and Emma regarding it
- Fanny was subsequently "turned out of the house" and eventually married Solomon Custer, a non-member

---

## The Nauvoo Period Begins: Louisa Beaman (April 5, 1841)

- Marked the beginning of Nauvoo-era plural marriage
- Conducted under an elm tree
- Bride disguised in a coat and hat
- Officiated by Joseph B. Noble

---

## Emma Smith's Role

Emma's involvement was uniquely fraught:
- Eventually learned of and initially consented to plural marriage
- Directly facilitated the sealings to [[Emily Dow Partridge]] and [[Eliza Maria Partridge]]
- Withdrew her consent; reportedly destroyed a written copy of the revelation
- The Fosters and Laws — who became *Expositor* publishers — were personal witnesses to the domestic strain within the Smith household

## The *Nauvoo Expositor* Connection

The *Expositor's* publication on June 7, 1844 — and Smith's decision to destroy the press — was directly triggered by the plural marriage controversy. The publishers ([[William Law]], [[Robert D. Foster]], et al.) were not traditional anti-Mormons but insider reformers who had personally witnessed the practice.

See: [[Nauvoo Expositor]] · [[William Law]] · [[1845 Carthage Conspiracy Trial]]
