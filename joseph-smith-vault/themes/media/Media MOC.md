# 📰 Media MOC — Joseph Smith and the Frontier Press

> Smith was one of the first American religious leaders to experience the full force of the "penny press" — sensationalist, mass-circulation journalism prioritizing human interest over elite partisanship.

---

## Three Categories of Reportage

### Category 1: Neutral-to-Favorable National Press

| Reporter | Publication | Key Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| [[David Nye White]] | *Pittsburgh Weekly Gazette* | 1843 Nauvoo interview; First Vision account; secular professional observer |
| [[James Gordon Bennett]] | *New York Herald* | National legitimacy; Smith called coverage "fair" and "high-minded" |
| Matthew L. Davis | *New-York Spectator* | Covered Smith's 1840 Washington, D.C., address |
| Israel Daniel Rupp | *He Pasa Ekklesia* | Published Smith's vision account directly, word-for-word |

### Category 2: Local Investigative Critics

| Reporter | Publication | Key Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| [[Abner Cole]] | *Palmyra Reflector* | Pseudonym "Obadiah Dogberry"; earliest journalistic critic of Book of Mormon |
| [[Thomas C. Sharp]] | *Warsaw Signal* | Primary inciter; violent editorials; organized Anti-Mormon Political Party; indicted for murder |
| Thomas Gregg | *Warsaw Message* | Brother-in-law of Sharp; author of *The Prophet of Palmyra* (1890) |
| [[Eber D. Howe]] | *Painesville Telegraph* | Published *Mormonism Unvailed* (1834) — first major anti-Mormon book |

### Category 3: Internal / Apostate Reformers

| Reporter | Publication | Key Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| [[Sylvester Emmons]] | *Nauvoo Expositor* | Edited sole issue; triggered Smith's arrest and death |
| [[William Law]] | *Nauvoo Expositor* | Publisher; former First Presidency counselor |

## The "Media-Legal Cycle" (Core Mechanism)

From [[The Media the Law and the Prophet]]:

> Press → Public Opinion → Jury Pool / Mob Action → Legal Outcomes

| Cycle Stage | Press Actor | Legal Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| New York, 1820s | [[Abner Cole]] ridicule | 1830 "disorderly person" trials |
| Ohio, 1834 | [[Eber D. Howe]] / Hurlbut affidavits | National anti-Mormon stereotype |
| Illinois, 1844 | [[Thomas C. Sharp]] editorials | Assassination + 1845 acquittal of killers |

---

## The Penny Press Context

The mid-1840s was the transition from elite partisan sheets to mass-circulation "penny press." Key characteristics:
- Prioritized sensationalism and human interest
- Expanded readership beyond the educated elite
- Created national narratives from local stories
- Smith was among the first American religious leaders to face this new media environment
